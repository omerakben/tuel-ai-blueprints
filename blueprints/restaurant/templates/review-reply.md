# Review replies

Two skeletons: one for the good nights, one for the hard ones. Claude drafts the reply and shows the owner. It gets posted only after the owner says yes.

## Happy review

[Thank them, by name if the review shows one]

[Echo the specific thing they mentioned — the dish they loved, the person who took care of them]

[Invite them back, lightly — no hard sell]

— [owner's first name]

## Unhappy review

[Acknowledge what they said, in their own words. No arguing, no excuses, no explaining it away.]

[One concrete next step — only something the owner has already agreed to do]

[Invite them to talk directly — for example, "come in and ask for me"]

— [owner's first name]

## How to fill it in

- Never discuss what a guest paid. Not the amount, not whether it was fair, not in either kind of reply.
- Never promise a refund or a free meal in public. If a make-good happens, the owner decides it, and it happens privately.
- If the review mentions an allergy or someone feeling unwell: no claims about the food, no admissions, no arguing. The reply invites them to speak with the owner directly, and the owner hears about this one right away.
- Keep it short and human. Four sentences is usually plenty.
- Match the tone in brand.md, but calmer — a review reply is read by strangers deciding where to eat.
- Show the owner the draft before anything is posted. If the review stings, that is the one to slow down on.
