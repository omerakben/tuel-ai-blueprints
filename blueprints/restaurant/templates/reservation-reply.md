# Reservation reply

A short, warm reply to someone asking for a table. Claude drafts it from options the owner gave and shows the owner. Only the owner sends it — and only the owner's book confirms anything.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name, if they gave one]! Thanks for asking about [day] for [party size]. We can offer [option one] or [option two] — would either work for you? [One line only if a house rule applies, like a heads-up for big groups, in the owner's words from policies.md.] [Warm close] — [owner's or restaurant's name]

## How to fill it in

- Every time offered comes from the owner, looking at their book. Never propose a time the owner didn't give.
- Offer, don't confirm. "We can offer" and "would that work?" — never "you're booked" or "see you then". The owner confirms after it's written in the book.
- If the guest asked about allergens or whether a dish is safe for them, the reply says to speak with the owner directly, and nothing more on that topic.
- Three or four sentences. It should read like the owner typed it between tables.
- Show the owner the exact text before it is sent. They send it. A past yes does not cover this one.
