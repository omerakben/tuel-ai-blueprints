# Draft a review reply

You paste in a review, and Claude drafts a reply in your house voice for you to post — the good ones and the ones that sting.

## When to run this

- A new review just landed on Google or Yelp.
- A rough one came in and you'd rather see a calm draft than type one while your blood is up.
- An older review has sat unanswered and it bothers you.
- You want your replies to sound like one voice across the year.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how the restaurant talks, so the reply sounds like you and not like a form letter.
- business/staff.md — to thank the right person when the review names one.
- business/services.md — to get dish names right.
- business/policies.md — your hard limits, in case the review touches on no-shows or big parties.
- templates/review-reply.md — the shape each reply starts from.

You paste or say: the full review text, which site it's from, the star rating if there is one, and anything you remember about that visit. Claude never needs your Google or Yelp password. It works only with the text you paste.

## Steps

1. Paste the review and tell Claude which site it came from.
2. Claude reads it and takes its temperature. If anything is unclear, like which night or which dish, Claude asks you before writing a word.
3. For a happy review, Claude drafts a short thank-you that echoes the dish or the night they praised, using only names that match your business files, and invites them back.
4. For an unhappy or mixed review, Claude drafts a calm reply that thanks them for telling you and moves the conversation off the page: the draft invites them to speak with you directly. It does not argue and it does not make excuses. It never offers a refund or a free meal unless you decided that yourself for this exact review — then your exact offer goes in word for word.
5. If the review mentions an allergy or says someone felt unwell, the draft makes no claims about the food and no admissions. It invites the guest to speak with you directly, and Claude flags the review to you as one that may need more than a reply.
6. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Ask for changes as many times as you like — a shorter version, a warmer one. Nothing moves until you say the draft is good.
7. You post the reply on the site yourself. Claude never posts it for you, and it saves a copy to reports/ so there's a record of what was said.

## What you get

A ready-to-post reply, saved as reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md, one file per review. The file holds the original review, which site it came from, the reply you approved, and whether you posted it — so the next time a similar one lands, Claude can match the tone you liked.

## Never

- Never drafts a fake review, and never writes anything meant to look like it came from a guest.
- Never offers a refund, a free meal, or a discount in a reply unless you decided that for this exact review.
- Never suggests paying or rewarding anyone for a review, and never offers anything in exchange for changing or removing one.
- Never confirms in public what a guest ate, when they visited, or who they were with — that stays between you and them.
- Never makes an allergen or food-safety claim in a reply, in either direction. Those conversations happen with you directly, and some need a professional's eyes before anything is said.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
