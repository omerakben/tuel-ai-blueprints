# Draft a reservation reply

A guest wants a table. You paste in their request, tell Claude what you can offer, and get a reply ready to send — the book stays in your hands, and only you confirm.

## When to run this

- A reservation request lands: a text, a voicemail you jot down, an email, a social message.
- Someone asks to move or cancel a booking. Treat the new time like a fresh request and run the same steps.
- A guest asks something alongside the booking, like whether you can seat a party of ten.

## What Claude reads first

- business/profile.md — your hours, so a reply never offers a time you're closed.
- business/policies.md — your rules for large parties, holding tables, and no-shows.
- business/clients.md — in case the guest is a regular with a usual table.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the reply sounds like you.
- templates/reservation-reply.md — the shape each reply starts from.

And one thing only you have: your book. Claude cannot see it and never guesses at it. You say which times you can offer — that is the whole game.

## Steps

1. Paste the request, or describe the call in your own words.
2. Claude repeats back what it heard: how many people, which day, roughly what time, and a name if one was given. If a piece is missing, Claude stops and asks you — or the draft asks the guest.
3. Claude asks you which times you can offer. Look at your book and name one or two. Claude never proposes a time you did not give it.
4. Claude checks business/policies.md. If the party is big enough to trip a house rule, the draft says so in your words.
5. Claude drafts the reply from templates/reservation-reply.md: warm, short, offering your options as options — never as a done deal. It shows you the draft and stops there. You send it from your own phone or account, or ask for changes and Claude redrafts.
6. The guest picks a time and you write it in your book. Tell Claude once it's in — until then, Claude calls the time "offered", never "booked" or "confirmed".
7. If the guest mentioned something worth keeping, like an anniversary or a usual table, Claude offers one line for business/clients.md, shows you the exact words, and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

- The reply, ready for you to copy and send yourself.
- A short note saved as reports/reservation-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md holding the request, the options you offered, and the draft you approved — handy when the guest replies two days later.

## Never

- Never tells a guest a table is confirmed or held. Availability lives in your book, not in Claude, and only you confirm.
- Never invents an open time, a price, or a policy. If it is not in your files or in what you just said, Claude asks.
- Never sends the reply itself — every message goes out from your hands.
- Never answers an allergen or dietary question in a reply. If a guest asks whether a dish is safe for them, the draft invites them to speak with you directly, and that is all it says on the subject.
- Never takes card details, and never promises anything that costs money — a deposit waived, a round on the house, an exception — without your fresh decision.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
