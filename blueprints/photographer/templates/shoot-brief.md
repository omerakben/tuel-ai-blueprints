# Shoot brief

The one page the owner glances at before a shoot. Built from the booking, the client's own words, and the owner's notes — never from guesses.

## The skeleton

**Shoot:** [kind of session] for [client names]

**When:** [date and start time, from the booking]

**Where:** [location, from the booking — with a reminder line if the owner still needs to check permission there]

**What they're dreaming of:**
[The client's wishes in their own words, plus anything the owner wants to try]

**Timing notes:**
[When the light is right, when kids fade, when the venue opens — exactly as the owner said]

**What to pack:**
[Gear notes in the owner's words — bodies, lenses, batteries, anything to rent or charge]

**Day-before checks:**
[Anything to confirm with the client, and the location-permission line if it's still open]

## How to fill it in

- Fixed facts — names, date, time, place — come from the proposal in reports/, from clients.md, or from the owner. A missing fact becomes a question on the page, not a blank filled by guessing.
- Timing and gear are the owner's words written down. Claude doesn't work out sun times or invent a pack list.
- Location permission is the owner's to check. The brief reminds; it never answers.
- Keep it to one page. If it doesn't fit, it's a plan, not a brief.
