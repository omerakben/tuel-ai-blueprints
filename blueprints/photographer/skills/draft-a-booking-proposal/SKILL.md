# Booking proposals

Once you say the date works, Claude drafts the proposal — the package, what's
included, and your deposit line — ready for you to send. Your send is what books it.

## When to run this

After an inquiry warms up: the client likes a package, they've named a date, and
you've said the date works. It picks up where skills/draft-an-inquiry-reply/ left off.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — the package by name, its price, and what's included.
- business/policies.md — the deposit line and how a booking becomes official.
- business/brand.md — your voice.
- templates/booking-proposal.md — the shape the proposal starts from.
- The inquiry thread in reports/, if there is one.

## Steps

1. Confirm the pieces with the owner first: which package, which date, and that
   the owner has said the date works. Any of the three missing — ask, don't assume.
2. Pull the package, price, and what's included from services.md, word for word
   where it matters. Nothing gets added that the package doesn't carry.
3. Take the deposit line from policies.md: how much, when it's due, what it holds.
   If policies.md has no deposit policy yet, stop and ask the owner — never write
   a "usual" one.
4. Draft the proposal from templates/booking-proposal.md: warm and short, with the
   package, the date, and what happens next in the owner's own booking style — a
   deposit, an agreement, a plain yes.
5. Show the draft and wait. The owner edits or approves. The owner sends it, and
   the booking is only real when the owner says it is.
6. Save the proposal to reports/ so the shoot brief can build on it later.
7. If this client is new to business/clients.md, propose their line and wait for
   a yes before writing it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, named like reports/booking-proposal-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The proposal, ready to copy and send.
- The facts it used and where each came from — the package from services.md, the
  deposit line from policies.md, the date from the owner.
- Anything still open, one question per line.

## Never

- Never confirm a booking. The owner's send, and the client's yes, make it real.
- Never invent a deposit amount, a due date, or what a deposit holds. That comes
  from policies.md or the owner, nowhere else.
- Never touch the agreement itself. Contracts and releases are between the owner
  and the client, on paper the owner chose.
- Never promise a delivery timeline or photo-usage terms that aren't in
  policies.md or the owner's own words.
- Never mark a date as held. Holding a date is a promise, and promises are the
  owner's.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
