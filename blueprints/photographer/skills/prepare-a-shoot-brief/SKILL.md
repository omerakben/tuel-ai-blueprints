# Shoot briefs

One calm page before each shoot: who's coming, where it is, what they're hoping
for, when the timing matters, and what to pack — all from what you've said.

## When to run this

A day or two before a shoot. It pairs well with the morning brief: run it the
evening before and tomorrow starts with the page already waiting in reports/.

## What Claude reads first

- The booking proposal or inquiry thread in reports/ — what was agreed.
- business/clients.md — names, what they booked before, what they loved.
- business/services.md — what the booked package includes.
- templates/shoot-brief.md — the shape the page takes.
- Your words in this chat — shot wishes, timing, gear notes.

## Steps

1. Gather the fixed facts: client names, the kind of shoot, the date and start
   time, and the location — all from the proposal, clients.md, or the owner.
   Anything missing goes on the brief as a question, never a guess.
2. Collect the shot wishes: what the client said they're dreaming of, plus
   anything the owner wants to try. Their words, kept as their words.
3. Ask the owner for timing notes — when the light is right, when kids fade, when
   the venue opens. Claude writes down what the owner says; it doesn't work out
   sun times on its own.
4. Ask for gear notes in the owner's words: what to pack for this one, what to
   charge, what to rent. If a pack list is saved in business/, start from it and
   let the owner trim.
5. Add the owner's check lines. Whether the location needs a permit or permission
   is the owner's to check — the brief carries the reminder, not the answer.
6. Show the one page and wait. The owner adds or cuts; then it's saved to reports/.

## What you get

One page in reports/, named like reports/shoot-brief-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- Who, what, where, when — with any gaps marked as questions for the owner.
- The shot wishes, in the client's and owner's own words.
- Timing and gear notes, exactly as the owner gave them.
- The owner's check lines: location permission, plus anything to confirm with the
  client the day before.

## Never

- Never invent an address, a start time, or a sunset. Fixed facts come from the
  proposal, clients.md, or the owner.
- Never answer the permit question. Whether a place needs permission to shoot is
  the owner's to check — the brief only reminds.
- Never add gear the owner didn't name, and never turn a wish list into a promise
  to the client.
- Never put anything private about the clients on the brief beyond what the day
  needs.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
