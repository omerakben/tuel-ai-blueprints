# What to collect, in order

## Control choices

- Name the person who approves decisions and outside actions.
- Confirm the local time zone and usual working days.
- List which files and connected tools Claude may read for routine work.
- Choose how long temporary exports in `inbox/` should be kept. Claude may flag old files but never deletes them.
- Name the accountant, lawyer, technician, or other qualified person who reviews work outside Claude's lane, when relevant.
- Choose one repeated job for the first Assisted routine. Record how it works today, its approved sources, a useful result, and a stop signal.
- Review saved evidence before advancing a routine. A stage change never authorizes sending, posting, paying, filing, signing, deciding, or deleting.

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Business name
- The kind of shoots that fill the calendar (families, weddings, headshots, brands) and the feel of the work in three words
- Main sessions or packages, each with price, what's included, and rough length
- Where the shoots happen (studio, on location, both) and the area covered
- Who's behind the camera — just the owner is fine; otherwise who second-shoots, edits, or assists
- How inquiries arrive (email, messages, a website form)
- What makes a booking official today (a deposit, a signed agreement, a yes)

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full package and price list (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- House policies: deposits, cancellations and reschedules (weather too), how many photos clients get and how long delivery takes, and what clients may do with their photos
- Past clients the owner wants remembered: what they booked, when the last shoot was, whether the owner says they agreed to sharing photos
- The lab and suppliers: who prints, who makes albums, where gear gets rented or repaired
- Logo and a few favorite photos, dropped into `assets/`
- The business's look and voice: three words, colors, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, Yelp) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- The usual pack list for each kind of shoot, if the owner wants it on shoot briefs
- Mini-session seasons and the busiest months, as the owner sees them
- The business's story — how it started, what it's known for
- How a second shooter or editor gets paid, only if the owner wants the month-end tidy-up to track it
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (insurance, licenses, the website)
