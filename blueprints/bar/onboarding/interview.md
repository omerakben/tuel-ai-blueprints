# The first chat

This is the script for getting to know the bar. It runs the first time the owner says hello, and picks up where it left off any time after.

## How to run it

- One question at a time. Never two. Wait for the answer.
- Plain words only. Nothing technical, ever.
- Reflect back before saving: "So that's [what you heard] — did I get that right?"
- Follow the owner. If they jump ahead to what's bugging them, go there, then loop back.
- They can stop anytime. Note what's still missing (the tiers are in `onboarding/checklist.md`) and next time offer to pick up where you left off.
- Write nothing into `business/` without showing the exact lines and getting a yes.

## Opening

Say hello like a person. Then, in your own words:

> I'd love to get to know the bar — a few quick questions, one at a time, maybe twenty minutes. By the end you'll have your bar's profile saved and a ready-to-post promo for tonight, or for the next night something's on. And anything I ever draft, you see before it goes anywhere. Ready?

## Part 1 — the essentials

Ask these one at a time, in your own words, skipping anything the owner already said:

1. What's the bar called?
2. What kind of place is it — sports bar, dive, cocktail spot, a neighborhood local, a bit of everything?
3. If you had to describe the feel of the place in three words, what would they be?
4. What days and hours are you open?
5. What do people mostly drink here — the pours that carry the week? Take them one at a time: what it's called, what it costs, whether it's a draft, a bottle, or a cocktail.
   - If they have a drink list: "If you've got a menu handy — a photo or a file is fine — drop it in the `assets` folder or paste it here and I'll sort it out." Structure whatever arrives into a clean list and confirm it line by line. Messy is fine; guessing is not.
6. Any food — a full kitchen, a few snacks, or none? None is a fine answer.
7. Who works the bar? Just you? That's fine. Otherwise, one person at a time: their name, what nights they're on, what they're great at.
8. What's the rhythm of the week — which nights have something on (games, live music, trivia, karaoke) and which nights run quiet?
9. When something's happening tonight, how do people usually find out — Instagram, Facebook, a chalkboard out front, word of mouth?

After each answer, a short reflect-back. After all nine, show the drafted `business/profile.md`, `business/services.md`, and `business/staff.md` — the actual lines — and ask for the go-ahead to save.

## Part 2 — the first win

Right away, same session:

1. Ask what's on tonight — a game on the screens, live music, a special the owner wants to run. If tonight is quiet, ask for the next night something's happening.
2. Get the facts that make the post true: what it is, when it starts, and — if it's a special — what it costs. If any of those is missing, stop and ask. Never guess a kickoff time or a price, and never write around a missing one.
3. Draft the post with `templates/event-post.md` and the feel-words from question 3.
4. Save it to `reports/` and show it: "Here's tonight's post, ready when you are — want changes?"

The session must not end without something real in `reports/` that the owner can use today.

## Part 3 — switching on the rhythm

Offer the two daily helpers one at a time, and wait for an answer between them.

First:

> Want a two-minute rundown before you open — what's on tonight, who's behind the bar, anything running low or waiting on you?

If yes, walk them through creating that scheduled task using the exact prompt in `schedules/morning-brief.md`. Then:

> And a close-out after last call — tallies the till and tips, so nothing nags at you overnight?

If yes, same walk-through with `schedules/end-of-day.md`. If either is a no, no pressure — they can just ask any afternoon or evening.

Also offer, once:

> Want me to save a short note so every future chat already knows the bar? I'll show you the exact lines first.

The lines live at the end of `CLAUDE.md`, under "The short version". Show them, then save only on a yes.

## Closing

Three lines, warm:
- What got saved, and that they can change any of it by just saying so.
- What you'd love to learn next time (pull from Tier 2 of the checklist — house rules, regulars, distributors).
- That they can come back anytime and just talk to you like a person, because that's what works.
