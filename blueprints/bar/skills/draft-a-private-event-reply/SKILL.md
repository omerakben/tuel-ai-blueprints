# Draft a private event reply

Someone wants to book the bar for a birthday, a work thing, a watch party. You paste their message, and Claude drafts a warm reply that gathers the details — without promising a date or a price.

## When to run this

- A party inquiry just landed in your email, your messages, or a comment.
- You've been sitting on an inquiry for two days because you're not sure what to say.
- You want every inquiry answered the same warm way, whoever's behind the bar.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how the bar talks, so the reply sounds like you.
- business/profile.md — the room: what the space is, roughly what it fits.
- business/policies.md — your private-party rules, if you've written them down: deposits, minimums, who says yes.
- templates/private-event-reply.md — the shape the reply starts from.

From you: the inquiry itself, pasted in full, and anything you already know about that date or that person.

## Steps

1. Paste the inquiry. Claude reads it and pulls out what's already answered — maybe they said the date, maybe the headcount.
2. Claude drafts a warm reply from templates/private-event-reply.md that thanks them and asks for what's still missing: the date they want, how many people, and a rough budget for food and drinks. Only the missing pieces get asked — nobody likes answering the same question twice.
3. The draft never says the date is free, never quotes a price or a minimum, and never says yes. Availability and money are yours. If your policies.md lists a deposit or a minimum, Claude may mention that one exists — never the amount, and never as agreed.
4. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Adjust it as many times as you like.
5. You send the reply from your own email or account. Claude never sends it.
6. Claude saves a copy to reports/ so the thread has a paper trail. When they write back with the details, run this playbook again and the next draft picks up where this one left off — and the decision to confirm stays yours, made fresh.

## What you get

One file per inquiry, saved as reports/private-event-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the original inquiry, the reply you approved, and a short list of what's still unknown — date, headcount, budget — so you can see at a glance what the next message needs to settle.

## Never

- Never confirm a date, a time, or the room without the owner saying so for that exact event.
- Never quote a price, a deposit, a minimum, or a package. If the inquiry asks about cost, the draft says the owner will follow up once the date and headcount are set.
- Never take or promise money in any form.
- Never write anything aimed at minors or that encourages heavy or fast drinking — a party reply talks about the room, the night, and the food, not open bars that never close. Who gets served on the night stays a call made at the bar, and it never appears in a reply.
- Never invent details about the space, the menu, or what's possible. If it isn't in business/ or something the owner said, ask.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
