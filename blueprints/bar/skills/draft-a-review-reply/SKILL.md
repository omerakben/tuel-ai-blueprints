# Draft a review reply

You paste in a review from Google or Yelp, and Claude drafts a calm reply in your bar's own voice for you to post.

## When to run this

- A new review just landed on Google or Yelp.
- A rough review came in and you would rather see a calm draft than type one while annoyed.
- An older review has sat unanswered and it bothers you.
- You want your replies to sound like one voice, not five different moods.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how your bar talks, so the reply sounds like you and not like a form letter.
- business/staff.md — to name the right bartender when the review mentions one.
- business/services.md — to get drink and menu names right.
- business/policies.md — your house rules, in case the review touches on tabs or parties.
- templates/review-reply.md — the shape each reply starts from.

You paste or say: the full review text, which site it is from, the star rating if there is one, and anything you remember about that night. If a detail is missing, Claude asks you rather than guessing.

Claude never needs your Google or Yelp password. It works only with the text you paste.

## Steps

1. Paste the review and tell Claude which site it came from.
2. Claude reads it and takes its temperature. If anything is unclear, like who was working or what the complaint is really about, Claude asks you before writing a word.
3. For a happy review, Claude drafts a short thank-you that echoes the specific thing they loved — the pour, the music, the bartender by name if it matches your business files — and invites them back, lightly.
4. For an unhappy or mixed review, Claude drafts a calm reply that thanks them for telling you and moves the conversation off the page: the draft asks them to reach the bar at [your preferred contact] so you can talk it through directly. It does not argue and it does not make excuses. It never offers a free drink, a round, or anything else to make it up, unless you decide that yourself. If you do, tell Claude your exact offer and it goes into the draft word for word.
5. If the review is about being refused service, asked for ID, or cut off, the draft stays especially short: thank them for the note and invite them to talk directly. Claude never explains, defends, or second-guesses that call in public — it was made by a person at the bar, and it stays there.
6. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Ask for changes as many times as you like — a shorter version, a warmer one. Nothing moves until you say the draft is good.
7. You post the reply on Google or Yelp yourself. Claude never posts it for you.
8. Claude saves a copy to reports/ so you have a record of what was said.

## What you get

A ready-to-post reply, saved as reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md, one file per review. The file holds the original review, which site it came from, the reply you approved, and whether you posted it — so the next time a similar review lands, Claude can match the tone you liked.

## Never

- Never draft a fake review, and never write anything meant to look like it came from a customer.
- Never offer a free drink, a discount, or a make-good in a reply unless you decided that for this exact review.
- Never suggest paying or rewarding anyone for a review, and never offer anything in exchange for changing or removing one.
- Never discuss an ID check, a refusal, or someone being cut off in public — those calls belong to the people at the bar, and the reply invites the reviewer to talk directly instead.
- Never confirm in public who was in, what anyone drank, or how much — that stays between you and them.
- Never write a reply that jokes about heavy drinking or leans on drinking-game language, even if the review does.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
