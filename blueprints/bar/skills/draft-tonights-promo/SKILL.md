# Draft tonight's promo

Turns what's actually happening tonight — a game on the screens, live music, a special you name — into a ready-to-post caption. You post it; Claude never does.

## When to run this

- Something's on tonight and you haven't told anyone yet.
- The afternoon lull, phone in hand, and you want the post done in two minutes.
- The weekly events plan named tonight's happening and it's time to write the words.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how the bar talks, so the post sounds like you.
- business/profile.md — the bar's name, hours, and how people find you.
- business/services.md — real prices, so a special is never guessed.
- templates/event-post.md — the shape the post starts from.
- reports/ — this week's events plan, if one exists.

From you: tonight's happening. Three facts make a post true: what it is, when it starts, and — if it's a special — what it costs.

## Steps

1. Ask what's on tonight if you haven't said. A post needs a real happening. Claude never invents a game, a band, or a special.
2. Check the facts: the thing, the time, the price if there's a special. If any one is missing, Claude stops and asks. No guessed kickoff times, no writing around a missing price with "great deals".
3. Draft one caption from templates/event-post.md, in the voice from business/brand.md. One post, one message — if there are two things to say, that's two posts.
4. Show you the draft and wait. Ask for shorter, warmer, drier — as many passes as you like. Nothing moves until you say it's good.
5. Save the version you approved to reports/. You copy it into Instagram or Facebook, or chalk it on the board, whenever you're ready.

## What you get

One file, reports/tonights-promo-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the caption you approved, plus a line noting the happening, the time, and the price it's built on — so if plans change, you can spot it at a glance.

## Never

- Never invent an event, a start time, a band name, or a price. A missing fact stops the draft; Claude asks.
- Never write anything aimed at minors, and never encourage heavy or fast drinking — no drinking-game lines, no all-you-can-drink framing, nothing that pushes anyone to drink more or faster.
- Never present a happy-hour or discount offer as legal or final. What's allowed varies by town; the owner decides what runs and says so fresh each time.
- Never mention ID checks or who gets served. Those calls happen at the door and the bar, between people — they don't belong in a post.
- Never post anywhere. The caption waits in reports/ until you paste it yourself.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
