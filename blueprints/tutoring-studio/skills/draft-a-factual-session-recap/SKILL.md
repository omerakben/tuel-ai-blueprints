# Session recaps

After a session, Claude turns the tutor's notes into a short factual recap for the parent: what was covered, what was practiced, what's next. You send it.

## When to run this

- A session just ended and the family expects a note.
- The end-of-day close turned up recaps still owed.
- A parent asked how sessions are going and you want a clean, factual answer to build from.

## What Claude reads first

- business/clients.md — who to write to for this family, and the goals as the family stated them.
- business/brand.md — how the studio sounds, so the recap reads like you.
- templates/recap-note.md — the shape every recap follows.
- The tutor's notes for this session. Paste them in or say them out loud — rough is fine. No notes, no recap: Claude never reconstructs a session it wasn't told about.

## Steps

1. Share the tutor's notes — what was worked on, what the student practiced, what's planned next.
2. Claude repeats back what it heard and asks about anything unclear. A gap in the notes becomes a question to you, never a filler sentence.
3. Claude drafts the recap from templates/recap-note.md: what was covered, what was practiced, what's next. Facts only. The tutor's observations stay the tutor's, worded as what happened — "we spent extra time on fractions", not a verdict on the student.
4. If a note leans toward a judgment of ability or a prediction, Claude turns it into a plain fact or asks you how to phrase it. If it can't be said as a fact, it stays out.
5. Claude addresses the recap to the parent or guardian named in clients.md — or the student themselves only if they're an adult.
6. Claude shows you the draft and stops there. You send it, or ask for changes.
7. Claude saves the recap to reports/ so next session's "what's next" has a record to stand on.
8. If the notes held something durable — a topic to keep practicing, a change in rhythm — Claude offers the exact line for business/clients.md and waits for your yes.

## What you get

One file saved as reports/session-recap-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the notes as you gave them, the recap you approved, and the "what's next" line to pick up next time.

## Never

- Never judge ability. No "she's behind", no "he's a natural" — what happened in the session, nothing about what it means.
- Never promise outcomes: no grade predictions, no test-score talk, no "on track for" a school or program.
- Never mention grades, test scores, diagnoses, accommodations, or anything from a student's school records — even if a note brushes past one, it stays out of the recap.
- Never write to the student unless the owner has a written policy saying so; recaps go to the parent or guardian.
- Never invent what happened. If the notes don't say it and you didn't say it, it isn't in the recap.
- If anything in the notes touches a student's safety, Claude flags it to you immediately and plainly — it never goes into a draft and is never handled quietly.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
