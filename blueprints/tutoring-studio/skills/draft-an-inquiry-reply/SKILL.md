# Inquiry replies

A parent asks about tutoring; Claude drafts the warm reply that answers their questions and gathers what the studio needs to know. You send it.

## When to run this

- A new inquiry lands — an email, a text, a message from a form, or notes from a phone call.
- A family you spoke to weeks ago writes back and the thread needs picking up.
- Someone asks about a subject or level and you want the reply to get the details right.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — your session types, rates, and lengths, so every answer is the real one.
- business/profile.md — where and when sessions happen, and how families book.
- business/staff.md — who tutors which subjects, so the reply never promises a subject nobody teaches.
- business/policies.md — cancellations, makeup sessions, how families pay.
- business/brand.md — how the studio sounds, so the reply reads like you wrote it.
- templates/inquiry-reply.md — the shape the reply starts from.

Then from you: the inquiry itself. Paste it in, or describe the call in your own words.

## Steps

1. Share the inquiry. Claude repeats back what it heard: what the parent asked, the subject if they named one, and anything they already told you about the student.
2. Claude answers only from your files. Rates come from services.md, days and places from profile.md, who-teaches-what from staff.md. If the reply needs a fact that isn't there — a rate, an opening, whether you even take that subject — Claude stops and asks you before drafting.
3. Claude drafts the reply from templates/inquiry-reply.md, in your voice, addressed to the parent or guardian. It answers what was asked and warmly gathers the three things the studio needs: the subject, the grade level, and what the family hopes to get out of tutoring.
4. Claude shows you the draft and stops there. You send it from your own email or phone, or say what to change and Claude redrafts.
5. Claude saves the run to reports/ so the thread is easy to pick back up when the parent replies.
6. If the inquiry held something durable — a family's name, a subject, a goal the parent stated — Claude offers the exact line for business/clients.md and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

One file saved as reports/inquiry-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the inquiry as you shared it, the reply you approved, and what's still waiting on the family.

## Never

- Never write to the student. The reply goes to the parent, the guardian, or an adult student — through the way that family reaches you.
- Never quote a rate, a day, or an opening that isn't in business/ or something you just said. Missing means ask, not improvise.
- Never promise results, a grade, or a spot in a school or program. The reply offers sessions, not outcomes.
- Never ask for or write down grades, test scores, diagnoses, or anything from a student's school records. Goals in the family's own words are enough.
- Never confirm a session time here — times come later, from the openings you list, through the session-options playbook.
- Never send the reply itself. Every message goes out from your hands.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
