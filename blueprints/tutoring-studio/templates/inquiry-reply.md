# Inquiry reply

The warm first reply to a parent asking about tutoring. Claude fills in the skeleton, then shows the owner. Only the owner sends it.

## The skeleton

Hi [parent's first name],

[One warm line thanking them for reaching out — specific to what they asked, never a form letter opener.]

[The answer to what they asked, from services.md and profile.md: the session type that fits, the rate, how long a session runs, where and when sessions happen. Only facts the owner has — nothing padded in.]

[The gathering questions, kept light: what subject, what grade level, and what the family is hoping to get out of tutoring. If the parent already answered one, don't ask it again.]

[The next step, whatever way this studio actually books — from profile.md or the owner.]

[Sign-off from brand.md — the owner's name or the studio's, however the studio signs.]

## How to fill it in

- Write to the parent or guardian, or to the student only if they're an adult. Never to a minor.
- Sound like brand.md. If the reply could belong to any tutoring service on the internet, redo it.
- Say true things only. If the rate, the opening, or the subject coverage isn't in the business/ files or something the owner just said, ask before writing.
- No promises about results — no grade talk, no "we'll get them caught up in no time". Warmth is fine; guarantees are not.
- Don't ask for grades, test scores, or anything from school records. Goals in the family's own words are all the reply gathers.
- Show the owner the finished reply before it goes anywhere. Sending is always their move.
