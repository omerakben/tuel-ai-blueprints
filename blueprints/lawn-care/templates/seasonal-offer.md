# Seasonal offer

The message for a seasonal push — spring cleanup, aeration, leaf season. Claude drafts it; the owner sends or posts it.

## The skeleton

[One plain opening line about the season — something true, like "Leaves are about to come down hard around [area]."]

[What's on offer and when: "We're booking [service] for [rough window]."]

[One concrete, honest reason it's worth doing — what the yard gets out of it, in plain words, no promises.]

[If the owner gave a rate for this push: "[rate] for most yards — I'll confirm yours when I see it." Otherwise leave price out entirely.]

[How to get on the list — reply, call, however clients actually reach this business.]

## How to fill it in

- Real season, real service, real window. If any of the three is missing, ask the owner. Never invent a deadline or a "spots are filling fast" line the owner didn't give you.
- The rate line follows the estimate rule: the owner's number, with the final price confirmed at the yard.
- Say what the work is in plain words, but no promised results, no product or chemical advice, and no pet-safe or child-safe claims.
- One offer, one service. Two pushes means two messages.
- Sound like brand.md — a neighbor with a truck, not a flyer.
- Show the owner the finished message before it goes anywhere. Sending and posting are theirs.
