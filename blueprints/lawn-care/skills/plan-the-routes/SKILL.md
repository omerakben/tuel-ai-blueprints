# Plan the routes

Lay out the week's visits in a sensible drive order — a proposal for you to shuffle,
not a plan anyone gets told about.

## When to run this

Run it when you plan the week ahead — it's the heart of the weekly rhythm hour
(workflows/weekly-routes.md). It's also handy mid-week, when rain knocks a day out
and the stops need to land somewhere else.

## What Claude reads first

- business/clients.md — your regulars, their rhythm, and roughly where they are.
- business/staff.md — who's working which days, if there's a crew.
- business/profile.md — the area you cover.
- templates/route-sheet.md — the shape each day's sheet takes.
- The current week's plan in reports/, if one exists.

## Steps

1. Get the week's list. Claude starts from the yards due by their rhythm in clients.md,
   plus any one-off jobs you name. Anyone whose rhythm isn't written down goes on a
   "need your call" list with a question — never a guess.
2. You settle the list. Cross off, add, push a yard to next week. Nothing gets routed
   until you've settled who's on.
3. Group by day and area. Claude proposes which yards share a day, keeping nearby stops
   together and respecting anything in clients.md like "Fridays only".
4. Order each day into a sheet from templates/route-sheet.md: stop order, service,
   rough time, and the yard notes worth having in the truck.
5. Show you the whole week and wait. You know the roads, the traffic, and where the
   dump run fits — reorder anything, and Claude redoes the sheets.
6. Save the plan to reports/. If you want day-before texts, that's the confirmation
   playbook — Claude offers it, you decide.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like
reports/route-plan-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- One route sheet per working day: stops in order, service, rough time, notes.
- The "need your call" list — yards Claude couldn't place without asking you.
- The leftovers: yards due but not booked, one-off jobs without a day yet.

## Never

- This plan is internal. No client is told a day or a time from it — that only happens
  through a confirmation text you approved and sent yourself.
- Never guess who's due. If clients.md doesn't say, Claude asks.
- Weather calls are yours. A rain shuffle is proposed as a draft, never assumed, and
  no draft promises a day you haven't confirmed.
- Property access is yours too. The sheet can carry a saved note like "gate on the
  left"; it never records codes and never decides how to get into a yard.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
