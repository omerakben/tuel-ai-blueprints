# Draft a yard estimate

Turn a new inquiry into a written estimate built from your own rates, ready for you to
send — with the final number staying your call until you've seen the property.

## When to run this

Run it the moment an inquiry comes in, while the details are fresh. It also works for
pricing extra work for a regular — aeration for a mowing client, a cleanup for the
neighbor of one.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — your rates, per visit or per job. Every number in the estimate
  comes from here or from you.
- business/profile.md — the area you cover, so the draft never offers a town you don't work.
- business/policies.md — payment terms and anything you always ask for up front.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the estimate sounds like you.
- templates/estimate.md — the shape the draft takes.

## Steps

1. Gather the yard facts, one question at a time: rough size, condition (overgrown,
   tidy, sloped, fenced), what the client wants done, and how often. What you or the
   client said is the whole picture — Claude never fills a gap with a guess.
2. Match the request to your services. Each piece of work maps to a line in services.md.
   If a requested service has no saved rate, Claude stops and asks you for the number.
   If you don't have one yet, the draft carries [your rate] in its place.
3. Build the draft from templates/estimate.md, in your voice. It lists the work, the
   rate for each piece, and the visit rhythm, and it says plainly that the final price
   comes after you've seen the property.
4. Flag anything unusual back to you as a question — a steep slope, a huge lot, a
   "the last guy quit on us" story — rather than pricing around it.
5. Show you the draft and wait. You edit, or say yes. A yes confirms the words;
   you're the one who sends it.
6. Save it to reports/ so you can copy it into a text or an email when you're ready.
7. If a new durable fact came up — say, a service you price all the time but never
   wrote down — Claude proposes the services.md line and waits for your yes to write it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like
reports/yard-estimate-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The estimate, ready to copy and send.
- The facts it was built from, so you can sanity-check at a glance.
- Any open questions — a missing rate, a detail worth checking at the yard.

## Never

- Never invent a rate, a discount, or a start date. If it's not in services.md or
  something you just said, Claude asks.
- Never call it a final price. Every estimate says the final number is your call after
  you've seen the yard.
- Never promise a day you haven't confirmed. The draft says [day] until you fill it.
- Never add pesticide, herbicide, or fertilizer advice, and never write pet-safe or
  child-safe re-entry lines. Product and licensing questions are yours.
- Never a guaranteed result. "Greenest lawn on the block, or free" never makes a draft.
- Never send the estimate. It waits in reports/ until you send it yourself.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
