# Draft a seasonal service offer

When a season turns, draft the push — spring cleanup, aeration, leaf season — to your
client list, ready for you to send.

## When to run this

When you feel the season turning and want the calendar filled before it does: spring
cleanups in late winter, aeration when you know it's coming, leaf work before the first
big drop. You call the timing — you know your area's rhythm better than any calendar.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — the seasonal service and its rate. The offer only ever
  mentions work you actually sell.
- business/clients.md — your regulars, what they usually take, and who welcomes messages.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the offer sounds like you and not like a flyer.
- templates/seasonal-offer.md — the shape the message starts from.

## Steps

1. You name the push: which season, which service, and the rough window you want to
   book. If the service isn't in services.md yet, Claude asks for the rate before
   writing a word.
2. Build the list. Claude proposes who should hear about it from clients.md — regulars
   who took this work before, mowing clients who might add it — and shows you the list
   first. Anyone who asked not to hear from you stays off.
3. Draft the offer from templates/seasonal-offer.md: one general version, plus a
   personal line for any regular you want to nudge by name. Real window, plain reasons,
   no invented deadline, no "only two spots left" pressure you didn't ask for.
4. Show you the whole batch and wait. You edit, cut, and approve by name.
5. You send it — texts from your phone, emails from your account, or a post you paste
   yourself. Claude sends and posts nothing.
6. Save it all to reports/ so you can track who bit.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like
reports/seasonal-offer-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The general offer, ready to copy.
- Personal versions for the regulars you approved.
- The send list you approved, and the skipped list with the reason for each.

## Never

- No guaranteed-results lines. "Greenest lawn on the block, or free" and all its
  cousins never make a draft.
- No pesticide, herbicide, or fertilizer advice, and no pet-safe or child-safe re-entry
  claims. An offer names the work, never the products or the chemistry.
- Never invent a price, a discount, or a deadline. If it's not in services.md or from
  you today, it's not in the offer.
- Never send or post the offer, and never set one up to go out later. You send every copy.
- Never message anyone who asked to be left alone, no matter how well the work fits
  their yard.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
