# What to collect, in order

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Business name
- The kind of work (weekly mowing and upkeep, landscaping jobs, a mix)
- How the owner wants to sound to clients, in three words
- The area covered (towns or neighborhoods)
- Who's on the crew (just the owner is fine), what each person is great at, and their days
- Main services with rate (per visit or per job) and rough time
- Roughly how many regular yards, and their usual rhythm
- How clients pay, and when
- How new clients find and reach the business today

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full rate sheet (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- House policies: rain days, skipped visits, cancellation notice, what happens when payment runs late
- The regulars: names the owner wants remembered, their yard in a line, usual service and rhythm, anything they always ask for
- Suppliers: where mulch, soil, and seed come from, who fixes the mowers, how orders get placed
- Logo and a few photos of the best yards, dropped into `assets/`
- The business's look and voice: three words, colors, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, Yelp, Nextdoor) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- The seasonal calendar as the owner sees it: when spring cleanups start, when aeration season lands, when the leaves come down
- Equipment worth tracking, and any service date the owner wants a nudge about
- Busiest and slowest stretches of the year, as the owner sees them
- The business's story — how it started, what it's known for
- How each crew member is paid (hourly, per job, a split), only if the owner wants the end-of-day close to track it
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (insurance, business license, truck registration)
