# Reply to a review

You paste in a review from Google or Yelp, and Claude drafts a reply in your salon's own voice for you to post.

## When to run this

- A new review just landed on Google or Yelp.
- A rough review came in and you would rather see a calm draft than type one while upset.
- An older review has sat unanswered and it bothers you.
- You want your replies to sound like one voice, not five different moods.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how your salon talks, so the reply sounds like you and not like a form letter.
- business/staff.md — to name the right stylist when the review mentions one.
- business/services.md — to get service names right.
- business/policies.md — your hard limits, in case the review touches on cancellations or deposits.
- templates/review-reply.md — the shape each reply starts from.

You paste or say: the full review text, which site it is from, the star rating if there is one, and anything you remember about that visit. If a detail is missing, Claude asks you rather than guessing.

Claude never needs your Google or Yelp password. It works only with the text you paste.

## Steps

1. Paste the review and tell Claude which site it came from.
2. Claude reads it and takes its temperature. If anything is unclear, like who served them or what they had done, Claude asks you before writing a word.
3. For a happy review, Claude drafts a short thank-you that mentions the stylist or service the reviewer praised, using only names that match your business files, and invites them back.
4. For an unhappy or mixed review, Claude drafts a calm reply that thanks them for telling you and moves the conversation off the page: the draft asks them to reach the salon at [your preferred contact] so you can talk it through directly. It does not argue and it does not make excuses. It never offers a refund or a redo, and it never promises anything else to make it up, unless you decide that yourself. If you do, tell Claude your exact offer and it goes into the draft word for word.
5. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Ask for changes as many times as you like — a shorter version, a warmer one. Nothing moves until you say the draft is good.
6. You post the reply on Google or Yelp yourself. Claude never posts it for you.
7. Claude saves a copy to reports/ so you have a record of what was said.
8. If the review points to something worth remembering, like a third complaint about waiting times, Claude proposes a note for your business files and writes it only if you agree.

## What you get

A ready-to-post reply, saved as reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md, one file per review. The file holds the original review, which site it came from, the reply you approved, and whether you posted it — so the next time a similar review lands, Claude can match the tone you liked.

## Never

- Never draft a fake review, and never write anything meant to look like it came from a customer.
- Never offer a refund, redo, discount, or free service in a reply unless you decided that for this exact review.
- Never suggest paying or rewarding anyone for a review, and never offer anything in exchange for changing or removing one.
- Never confirm in public what a reviewer had done, when they visited, or anything else from their file — that stays between you and them.
- Never answer a skin or scalp complaint with advice about the condition itself — the reply invites them to contact the salon, and a health worry belongs with a professional.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
