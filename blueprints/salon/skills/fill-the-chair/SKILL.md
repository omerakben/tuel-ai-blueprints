# Fill the chair

When the book has open slots today or this week, this playbook finds the fastest honest way to fill them and puts every draft in front of you before anything goes out.

## When to run this

- You look at today's book and see empty slots.
- A cancellation just opened up a chair.
- The week ahead looks thin and you want to get ahead of it.

## What Claude reads first

- Today's or this week's appointment book. Paste it in or read it out — whatever is easiest. If your booking tool is connected, say "check the book" and Claude reads back what it sees so you can confirm it's right.
- business/clients.md — your regulars, how often they usually come in, and any "do not message" notes.
- business/policies.md — which perks and discounts are already allowed, and your hard limits.
- business/services.md — what fits in each open slot, by length.
- business/staff.md — who works which days, so a day off is never treated as a gap.
- business/brand.md — your voice, so drafts sound like you.
- templates/promo-post.md and templates/sms-reminder.md — the starting shapes for the drafts.

## Steps

1. Confirm the gaps. Claude lists each open slot: the day and time, and whose chair it is. You confirm nothing is off — a lunch break or a walk-in buffer is not a gap.
2. Check what's allowed. Claude reads policies.md and tells you plainly which perks or discounts it may use in drafts. If a move would need a discount that isn't in policies.md, Claude stops and asks you. Your yes covers this one case only.
3. Line up the options. Claude proposes the moves that fit and recommends one, with a plain reason why:
   - A last-minute-opening post for your social pages, built from templates/promo-post.md.
   - Short personal texts to regulars who are due back, picked from clients.md and built from templates/sms-reminder.md.
   - A small perk that policies.md already allows, offered to the next booking that fits the slot.
4. You pick. Claude drafts only the move you choose, or more than one if you say so.
5. Review every draft. Claude shows you the post and each text one by one, with [client name] and [open time] filled in from the book. You say yes or no to each, or ask for a change. Claude never sends or posts anything; you send each one yourself once you're happy with it.
6. Save the record. Claude writes the gap list and the move you chose to reports/, along with every draft you approved.
7. Tidy the memory, with your okay. If something durable came up, like a regular who moved away or a new "text me anytime" note, Claude suggests the exact line to add to clients.md and waits for your yes before writing it.

## What you get

One short file saved as reports/fill-the-chair-[date].md. It holds:

- The gap list: each open slot and whose chair it was.
- The move you picked, and why.
- Every approved draft, word for word, ready to copy and send.
- A "sent?" line under each draft so you can tick off what actually went out.

## Never

- Never send a text or publish a post. Every message leaves by your hand, one at a time.
- Never invent a price or a discount. If it isn't in the business/ files or something you just said, Claude asks.
- Never text a client marked "do not message" in clients.md, or anyone who asked for space.
- Never promise one open slot to several people without saying so; drafts offer the time as "first to reply".
- Never reuse yesterday's yes. A fresh gap means a fresh draft and a fresh approval.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
