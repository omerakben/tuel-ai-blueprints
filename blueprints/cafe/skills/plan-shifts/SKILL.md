# Plan the shifts

A proposed coverage plan for next week, built from the days your crew usually works and whatever you tell Claude about the week ahead. A proposal only — every decision stays yours.

## When to run this

- Late in the week, when next week's plan is on your mind.
- Before a week with something unusual in it — a holiday, a market day, someone away.
- Any time the current plan stopped fitting and you want a fresh starting point.

## What Claude reads first

- business/staff.md — who's on the crew and the days each person usually works.
- business/profile.md — your opening days and hours, so the proposal covers what's actually open.
- Your notes for the week: who's away, what's coming up, anything you already know you want.

If it's just you, this playbook still works — it becomes a simple week plan around your own days.

## Steps

1. Ask about the week ahead: anyone away, any day that needs an extra pair of hands, anything unusual. One question at a time.
2. Build a proposed grid from business/staff.md only. If the file doesn't say someone works Tuesdays, Claude doesn't put them down for Tuesday — it leaves a gap and asks.
3. Mark every gap as an open question. A hole in the grid is something to ask you about, never something to fill by assumption.
4. Show you the proposal: each open day, who's proposed for it, and the open questions underneath. You move people around, fill gaps, or throw the whole thing out — it's a starting point, not a rota.
5. Once you've settled the plan, Claude drafts a short note per person from templates/shift-note.md with their days for the week. You send each note yourself, however you usually reach your crew.
6. Save it all as reports/shift-plan-YYYY-MM-DD.md.
7. If someone's usual days have changed for good, Claude proposes the exact new line for business/staff.md and waits for your yes before writing it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/shift-plan-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The proposed week: each open day, who's proposed for it, and the gaps still open.
- The open questions, one per line, so nothing gets silently assumed.
- One drafted note per person, ready for you to send once you've decided.

## Never

- Never infer someone's availability. The days written in staff.md and your own words are all Claude knows — everything else is a question.
- Never plan breaks, count hours toward overtime, or weigh anyone's workload. Those calls are yours.
- Never touch hiring, firing, pay, or anything like it — not in a plan, not in a note, not as a suggestion.
- Never present the proposal as decided. Nothing is a shift until you say it is and tell your crew.
- Never send a note to anyone. You send; Claude drafts.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
