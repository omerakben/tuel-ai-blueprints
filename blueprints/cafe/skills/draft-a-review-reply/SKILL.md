# Review replies

Paste in a review from Google or Yelp; Claude drafts a reply that sounds like your cafe, and you post it when you're happy.

## When to run this

- A new review landed — good or rough — and you want a reply worth reading.
- You're catching up on a backlog of reviews during a quiet afternoon.
- A review stung and you want a steady hand on the first draft.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how the cafe sounds, so the reply reads like you.
- business/policies.md — your rules on remakes and refunds, so no draft promises what you wouldn't.
- templates/review-reply.md — the two skeletons: one for the good days, one for the hard ones.

You paste the review, and mention anything Claude should know — what actually happened, or what you've already decided to do about it.

## Steps

1. Read the review together. Claude notes whether it's a happy one or a hard one and picks the matching skeleton from templates/review-reply.md.
2. Draft the reply in the cafe's voice: short, warm, specific to what the reviewer actually said. Four sentences is usually plenty.
3. Keep promises out of public. No refunds, remakes, or freebies in the reply unless you've already decided on one and said so.
4. If the review mentions an allergy, a reaction, or getting sick, the draft stays short and kind, makes no claims about the kitchen, and invites them to contact you directly. Those conversations happen with you, not in a comment thread.
5. Show you the draft and wait. You edit, or say yes. If the review stings, that's the one to slow down on.
6. Save it as reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md. You paste the reply onto Google or Yelp yourself — posting is always yours.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The review, kept with its reply so you can find the pair later.
- The finished reply, ready to copy and post.
- A private note of anything you decided — a remake offered, a follow-up to make — that stays in this folder.

## Never

- Never discuss what a customer paid — not the amount, not whether it was fair.
- Never promise a refund, a remake, or a freebie in public. If a make-good happens, you decide it, and it happens privately.
- Never make claims about allergens, what suits a diet, or food safety in a reply — not even to reassure. Those words are yours, said directly.
- Never argue with a reviewer or explain away what they felt.
- Never post the reply. It waits in reports/ until you paste it yourself.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
