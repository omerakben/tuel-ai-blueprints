# Job estimate

The written estimate a customer gets. Claude drafts it and shows the owner. Only the owner sends it.

## The skeleton

[Business name] — estimate

For: [customer name]
Job address: [address]
Date: [date]

What we'd do:
- [Plain scope line — the work as the customer would say it, for example "replace the leaking shutoff valve under the kitchen sink"]
- [Second scope line if the job has more parts to it]
- [What's not included, if that's worth saying — for example "patching the drywall after the repair"]

The numbers:

| Work | Price |
| --- | --- |
| [job or labor line, priced from services.md] | [price] |
| [parts — actual cost from the supply house, or "to be priced"] | [price or "to be priced"] |

[Deposit line, only if policies.md asks for one on jobs like this]

This is an estimate, not a final price. The final number is [owner's first name]'s call after seeing the job — if anything changes it, you'll hear about it before the work happens.

Good for [how long, from policies.md]. Questions? Just reply — happy to talk it through.

## How to fill it in

- Scope lines a customer can read without a dictionary. "Replace the wax ring and reset the toilet", not trade shorthand.
- Every price comes from services.md or the owner's own words in this chat. There is no other source.
- Parts you can't price yet say "to be priced" — never a guess dressed up as a number.
- No claims about code, permits, safety, or warranty anywhere in the estimate. If the owner wants a warranty line, the owner gives the words.
- The "estimate, not a final price" line stays in, every time.
- Show the owner the finished estimate before it goes anywhere. Sending is always their move.
