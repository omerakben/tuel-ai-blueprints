# Service request reply

The reply to a "my sink is leaking" message. It gathers what the owner needs to know and offers arrival windows the owner approved. Claude drafts; the owner sends.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name], thanks for reaching out — sorry you're dealing with that.

A few quick questions so [owner's first name] shows up ready:
- What exactly is happening — [dripping, not draining, no hot water — match their words]?
- Where in the [house / building] is it?
- Since when, and is it getting worse?

[Owner's first name] could come by [arrival window the owner approved — for example "Tuesday between mid-morning and lunch"]. Would that work, or is [second approved window] better?

[Only if the message might be urgent but is not clearly an emergency: "If water is actively spreading, don't wait on texts — call [owner's first name] right away."]

## How to fill it in

- Emergencies are different. A gas smell, a burst pipe, sewage backing up, water pouring in: the reply is the owner's saved emergency script from business/policies.md, word for word, and nothing else. No script saved yet? Ask the owner — never improvise one.
- No diagnosis. The reply never says what the problem is or what it will cost — it says [owner's first name] will take a look.
- No do-it-yourself steps for anything involving gas, sewage, electrical, water pressure, flooding, or the structure of the building. If the owner's saved script tells people where the shutoff is, those are the owner's words — add nothing to them.
- Arrival windows come from the owner in this chat. Never invent availability.
- Keep it short and human, like the owner typed it between jobs. If it sounds like a call center wrote it, redo it.
- Show the owner the exact reply before it is sent. They send it.
