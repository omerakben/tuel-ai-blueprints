# Weekly jobs hour

One hour a week to line up the work: last week closed out, this week put in order, estimates and reminders queued for your yes.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. A lot of owners use Sunday evening or Monday before the first stop. Say "let's do the weekly jobs hour" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's plan and the end-of-day reports in reports/: what got done, what rolled over, what got invoiced. If there's no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Gather this week. You list the jobs on your plate — new calls, rollovers, anything promised. Claude pulls promised windows from recent service replies in reports/ and asks you to confirm each one; nothing gets assumed.
3. Order the week. Claude runs the plan-the-week playbook: urgent and promised work first, the rest grouped by area, too-full days flagged. You move things until it looks right.
4. Queue the money side. Claude lists estimates still to send or chase, and any invoices you name as unpaid, then asks which nudges or reminders to draft now. Each draft follows its own playbook and comes back for your fresh yes.
5. Build the parts list. From the week's jobs, Claude drafts one pick-up list per day, built only from what you listed. If you want a supply-house order drafted, it waits for your yes — and you place it.
6. Save the hour. The plan, the flags, the approved drafts, and the parts lists go to reports/ in one file you can open from your phone all week.

## What you get

- The week, ordered and sanity-checked, saved as reports/weekly-jobs-[date].md.
- Every nudge and reminder you approved, ready to copy and send.
- A per-day parts pick-up list.
- Flags you can act on: too-full days, at-risk windows, estimates going stale.

## Never

- Claude never books a job, promises a window, sends a nudge, or places an order. Everything waits for your hands.
- Claude never invents a job length, a rate, or your availability. If it's not in the files and you didn't say it this hour, it's a question.
- A yes covers that exact item, this week. Last week's yes approves nothing today, and staying quiet on an item is a no.
- The plan is a proposal. The week belongs to you, not to the file.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
