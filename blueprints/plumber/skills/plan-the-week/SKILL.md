# Plan the week

You list the jobs; Claude proposes an order that puts urgent work first and keeps the driving sane. It's a proposal — the week is yours.

## When to run this

- Sunday evening or Monday morning, before the week starts moving.
- Mid-week, when a big job lands and the rest needs reshuffling.
- Any time promised windows and new calls stop fitting in your head.

## What Claude reads first

- What you list right now — the jobs on your plate, in any order, with whatever detail you have.
- business/profile.md — your working days, hours, and the area you cover.
- business/staff.md — who's available which days, if there's a crew.
- business/services.md — typical times for common jobs, to sanity-check each day.
- reports/ — service replies with promised windows, and last week's plan if there is one.

## Steps

1. List the week's jobs in your own words: the job, the town or neighborhood, roughly how long, how urgent. Rough is fine.
2. Claude asks about anything unclear — a job with no length, a promised window that isn't on the list, a day you're not working. Questions, never guesses.
3. Claude proposes the order: urgent and promised-window work first, then the rest grouped by area so you're not crossing town twice in a day. Anything that doesn't fit gets flagged, not shoehorned.
4. Each day gets a sanity check against the typical times in services.md. A day that adds up past your hours is flagged as too full, with a suggestion for what to move.
5. Claude shows you the proposed week and says why it's in that order. You move things; Claude reshuffles until it looks right to you.
6. The plan is saved to reports/. It's a proposal on paper — telling customers their windows is your move, one at a time.
7. If jobs need parts, Claude adds a pick-up list per day from what you told it, so the supply-house run happens once instead of every morning.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/week-plan-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The week, day by day: jobs in order, area groupings, rough times.
- Flags: too-full days, jobs that didn't fit, promised windows at risk.
- A parts pick-up list per day, built only from what you listed.

## Never

- Never book a job or tell a customer a window — the plan is a proposal until you act on it.
- Never guess how long a job takes. If services.md is silent and you didn't say, Claude asks.
- Never drop a job silently to make a day fit — anything that doesn't fit is flagged to you.
- Never treat the plan as fixed. New day, new facts, new plan — just ask again.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
