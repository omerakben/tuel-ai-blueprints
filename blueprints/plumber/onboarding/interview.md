# The first chat

This is the script for getting to know the business. It runs the first time the owner says hello, and picks up where it left off any time after.

## How to run it

- One question at a time. Never two. Wait for the answer.
- Plain words only. Nothing technical, ever.
- Reflect back before saving: "So that's [what you heard] — did I get that right?"
- Follow the owner. If they jump ahead to what's bugging them, go there, then loop back.
- They can stop anytime. Note what's still missing (the tiers are in `onboarding/checklist.md`) and next time offer to pick up where you left off.
- Write nothing into `business/` without showing the exact lines and getting a yes.

## Opening

Say hello like a person. Then, in your own words:

> I'd love to get to know the business — a few quick questions, one at a time, maybe twenty minutes. By the end you'll have your business profile saved and a real job estimate drafted and ready for your okay. And anything I ever draft, you see before it goes anywhere. Ready?

## Part 1 — the essentials

Ask these one at a time, in your own words, skipping anything the owner already said:

1. What's the business called?
2. What kind of work do you take on — repairs, installs, drain work, remodels, all of it?
3. What area do you cover — which towns or neighborhoods do you actually drive to?
4. What days and hours do you work, and do you take after-hours or emergency calls?
5. Is it just you, or is there a crew? Just you? That's fine — plenty of good plumbing businesses are one person and a van. If there's a crew, take them one person at a time: their name, what they're best at, what days they're in.
6. What do you charge? Start with the hourly rate and any service call fee, then the common jobs one at a time: what the job is, what you usually charge, roughly how long it takes. Then how you price parts — at cost, with a markup, from the supply-house receipt.
   - If they have a rate sheet: "If you've got a rate sheet handy — a photo or a file is fine — drop it in the `assets` folder or paste it here and I'll sort it out." Structure whatever arrives into a clean rate list and confirm it line by line. Messy is fine; guessing is not.
7. How do customers reach you today — calls, texts, a website form, word of mouth?
8. If you had to describe how you talk to customers in three words, what would they be?
9. Last one, and it matters. When a message sounds like an emergency — a gas smell, a burst pipe, water coming through a ceiling — what do you want the reply to say? I'll save your exact words, and those are the only words I'll ever use for emergency replies.

After each answer, a short reflect-back. After all nine, show the drafted `business/profile.md`, `business/services.md`, and `business/staff.md`, plus the emergency-script lines for `business/policies.md` — the actual lines — and ask for the go-ahead to save.

## Part 2 — the first win

Right away, same session:

1. Ask: "Got a job you need to price — something from today or this week? Tell me about it like you'd tell a friend: what needs doing, where, what you saw if you've been out there."
2. Draft the estimate with `skills/draft-a-job-estimate/` and `templates/estimate.md`, using the rates you just saved. If the scope is fuzzy or a rate is missing, stop and ask — never guess. The draft says plainly that the final price is the owner's call after seeing the job.
3. If there's no live job to price, draft a practice estimate for their most common job instead — real rates, with [square brackets] where the address and specifics would go. It becomes a head start they can adapt the next time the phone rings.
4. Save what you made to `reports/` and show it: "Here's your first estimate, ready when you are — want changes?"

The session must not end without something real in `reports/` that the owner can use today.

## Part 3 — switching on the rhythm

Offer the two daily helpers one at a time, and wait for an answer between them.

First:

> Want a two-minute morning brief before you load the van — today's jobs in order, parts to grab, anything waiting on you?

If yes, walk them through creating that scheduled task using the exact prompt in `schedules/morning-brief.md`. Then:

> And a close-out at the end of the day — what got done, what got collected, what rolls to tomorrow, so nothing nags at you overnight?

If yes, same walk-through with `schedules/end-of-day.md`. If either is a no, no pressure — they can just ask any morning or evening.

Also offer, once:

> Want me to save a short note so every future chat already knows the business? I'll show you the exact lines first.

The lines live at the end of `CLAUDE.md`, under "The short version". Show them, then save only on a yes.

## Closing

Three lines, warm:
- What got saved, and that they can change any of it by just saying so.
- What you'd love to learn next time (pull from Tier 2 of the checklist — deposits and payment terms, repeat customers, the supply house).
- That they can come back anytime and just talk to you like a person, because that's what works.
