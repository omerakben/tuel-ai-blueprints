# What to collect, in order

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Business name
- The kind of work taken on (repairs, installs, drain work, remodels) and how the owner talks to customers, in three words
- The area covered — the towns or neighborhoods the owner actually drives to
- Days and hours, and whether after-hours or emergency calls are taken
- Just the owner, or a crew — and if a crew, who does what and which days
- The hourly rate, any service call fee, common jobs with what they cost and roughly how long they take, and how parts are priced
- How customers reach the business today (calls, texts, a website form, word of mouth)
- The emergency script, in the owner's exact words

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full rate sheet (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- House policies: deposits on bigger jobs, payment terms, how long an estimate stays good, cancellations and no-shows
- Repeat customers and property managers: the property, past work done there, how they like to be reached
- Suppliers: the supply house, where specialty parts come from, and how orders get placed
- Logo and a few photos of finished work, dropped into `assets/`
- Where reviews land (Google, Yelp) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- What after-hours or emergency calls cost, if the owner charges differently for them
- Busiest and slowest seasons, as the owner sees them
- The story of the business — how it started, what it's known for
- Who usually pulls permits and how that tends to go (notes for the owner's memory only — drafts never claim permit status)
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (license, insurance, certifications)
