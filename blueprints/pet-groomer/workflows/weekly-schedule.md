# Weekly schedule hour

One hour a week to look at the week ahead — where it's full, where it's thin — and get every message drafted that keeps it moving. Claude drafts; you decide what goes out.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. Many groomers use Monday morning coffee or the quiet stretch after the last Saturday pickup. Say "let's do the week" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's plan in reports/ and checks what went out and what came of it. If there is no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Lay out the week. Paste the week's book, or read out the days. Claude lists each day plainly: full, comfortable, or thin, with the open slots named. If it can't see the book, it stops and asks — never a guessed week.
3. Build the due-back list. Claude runs the rebooking playbook (skills/draft-a-rebooking-reminder/) against business/clients.md: who's due, who needs your call, who's off-limits for texts.
4. Pick the moves together. For thin days, Claude proposes what fits and says why: reminder texts to due parents, offers to the waitlist for a specific open slot (skills/draft-a-cancellation-fill-message/), or simply leaving a light day light. You choose.
5. Draft the messages. Only the moves you picked, each from its template, each in your voice from business/brand.md. Names and rhythms come from clients.md only.
6. Approve the batch. Claude lays every draft out in one place. You go item by item: yes, no, or change it. Changed items come back for another look. An item you skip is a no. Only items with your fresh yes make the plan.
7. Make the week's plan. Claude ends with a short plan: each approved message, when you'll send it, and which day it's meant to fill. You do the sending. The plan and the approved drafts are saved to reports/.

## What you get

- The week at a glance: full days, thin days, and the open slots by name.
- The due-back list and every message you approved, ready to copy and send.
- A one-page plan saved as reports/weekly-schedule-[date].md, with the approved drafts underneath it.

## Never

- Claude never sends anything. Not one text leaves this folder without you.
- Claude never invents an opening, a price, or a due date. If it is not in your business/ files, your book, or something you just said, Claude asks.
- A yes covers that exact message, that week. Last week's yes approves nothing this week, and staying quiet on an item is a no.
- Never a text to a parent who didn't say texts are okay, or who asked to stop.
- Nothing veterinary in any message — no diagnosis, no treatment talk. A health worry goes to the vet, kindly, and never by text.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
