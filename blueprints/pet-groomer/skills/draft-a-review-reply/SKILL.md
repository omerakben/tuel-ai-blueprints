# Reply to a review

You paste in a review, and Claude drafts a warm reply in your own voice — naming the
pet if the reviewer did — for you to post.

## When to run this

- A new review just landed on Google or Yelp.
- A rough review came in and you would rather see a calm draft than type one upset.
- An older review has sat unanswered and it bothers you.
- You want your replies to sound like you on your best day, every time.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the reply sounds like you and not a form letter.
- business/staff.md — to name the right groomer when the review mentions one.
- business/services.md — to get groom names right.
- business/policies.md — your hard limits, in case the review touches fees or redos.
- templates/review-reply.md — the shape each reply starts from.

You paste or say: the full review text, which site it is from, the star rating if
there is one, and anything you remember about that visit. If a detail is missing,
Claude asks you rather than guessing.

Claude never needs your Google or Yelp password. It works only with the text you paste.

## Steps

1. Paste the review and tell Claude which site it came from.
2. Claude reads it and takes its temperature. If anything is unclear — which pet,
   which groomer, what was done — Claude asks you before writing a word.
3. For a happy review, Claude drafts a short thank-you that names the pet if the
   reviewer did, echoes the thing they loved, and invites them back lightly.
4. For an unhappy or mixed review, Claude drafts a calm reply that thanks them for
   telling you and moves the conversation off the page: the draft asks them to reach
   you at [your preferred contact] so you can talk it through directly. It does not
   argue and it does not make excuses. It never offers a refund or a redo unless you
   decide that yourself — if you do, tell Claude your exact offer and it goes in
   word for word.
5. If the review raises a health worry, the reply stays kind and out of the vet's
   lane: no explanation of what happened medically, no advice, no treatment talk —
   just the invitation to talk directly and, where it fits, a gentle "worth having
   your vet take a look".
6. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Ask for changes as many times as you like —
   a shorter version, a warmer one. Nothing moves until you say the draft is good.
7. You post the reply yourself. Claude never posts it for you.
8. Claude saves a copy to reports/ so you have a record of what was said.

## What you get

A ready-to-post reply, saved as reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md, one file per
review. The file holds the original review, which site it came from, the reply you
approved, and whether you posted it.

## Never

- Never draft a fake review, and never write anything meant to look like it came
  from a customer.
- Never offer a refund, redo, discount, or free groom in a reply unless you decided
  that for this exact review.
- Never suggest paying or rewarding anyone for a review, or offer anything in
  exchange for changing or removing one.
- Never confirm in public which pet visited, what was done, or anything else from
  their file — that stays between you and the parent.
- Never answer a health complaint with anything veterinary — no diagnosis, no
  medication talk, no treatment claims. The reply invites a direct conversation,
  and health worries go to the vet, kindly.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
