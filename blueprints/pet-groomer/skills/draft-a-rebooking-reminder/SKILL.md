# Rebooking reminders

Build your due-back list and draft one personal text per pet parent, ready for you to
send from your own phone.

## When to run this

Run it when the week ahead looks light, or on a steady rhythm like Monday morning.
It pairs well with the morning brief: you see which pups are due while you have your
coffee, and the reminders are ready before the first dog is on the table.

## What Claude reads first

- business/clients.md — your regulars, their last visits, their rhythms, and notes
  like "every six weeks" or "asked us not to text".
- business/services.md — the due-back rhythm for each groom, like how many weeks a
  full groom or a bath-and-tidy usually holds.
- business/policies.md — any limits you set on reaching out, like no texts after closing.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so every draft sounds like you.
- templates/reminder-text.md — the shape each text starts from.

If last-visit dates live in your booking tool, paste them in or read them out. Claude
also takes it plain: "it's been about seven weeks since [pet name] came in."

## Steps

1. Ask for anything missing. If clients.md has no last visit for a pet, Claude asks
   you rather than working around it. Never a guessed date, never a guessed rhythm.
2. Build the due-back list. For each regular, Claude compares the last visit to the
   rhythm for that pet's usual groom. The rhythm comes from services.md or a note in
   clients.md. If neither says, that pet goes on a "need your call" list with a
   question — never a guess.
3. Keep only the parents whose notes in clients.md say texts are okay. Anyone who
   ever asked to stop hearing from you stays off, no matter how due their pup looks.
4. Show you the list before writing a word. Cross off anyone you like; Claude waits
   for your go-ahead.
5. Draft one text per approved parent, starting from templates/reminder-text.md. Each
   one is short, sounds like you, names the pet and their usual groom, and ends with
   an easy out like "reply STOP and I won't text again". No two drafts read the same.
6. Show you the whole batch. You edit or cut any draft, then approve the keepers by
   name. You send them from your own phone — Claude never sends a text.
7. Save the report to reports/ so you can tick parents off as replies come in.
8. If something new came up, like a parent changing their rhythm or asking to stop,
   Claude proposes the update to clients.md and waits for your yes before writing it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like
reports/rebooking-reminders-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The due-back list: pet, parent, usual groom, last visit, and why they're due now.
- One draft text per approved parent, ready to copy and send.
- The "need your call" list: pets Claude couldn't place without asking you.
- A skipped list with the reason for each — no okay for texts, or asked to stop.

## Never

- Never guess a due-back rhythm or a last visit. If services.md and clients.md are
  both silent, ask.
- Never draft for a parent who didn't say texts are okay, or who ever said stop.
- Never send a text, and never set one up to go out later. Drafts live in the report;
  your phone does the sending.
- Never drop the easy way to say "stop texting me". Every draft carries one.
- Never bring a pet's health into a reminder — no skin, ear, lump, or behavior talk,
  no diagnosis, no treatment claims. If a note leans that way, keep the text to
  booking, and a worry like that goes to the vet, kindly, from the owner in person.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
