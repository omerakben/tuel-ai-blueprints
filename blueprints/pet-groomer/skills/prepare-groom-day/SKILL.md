# Prepare the groom day

Tomorrow's run sheet on one page: who's coming, coat notes, special asks — the page
you glance at with your coffee.

## When to run this

The evening before, or first thing in the morning. It pairs with the morning brief:
the brief points at the day, the run sheet holds the details.

## What Claude reads first

- Tomorrow's book. Paste it in, read it out, or save a copy in assets/ the night
  before. Claude cannot see your booking tool on its own — no book, no run sheet.
- business/clients.md — coat and cut notes, rhythms, and how each pet does on the
  table, in the parent's or your own words.
- business/services.md — how long each groom runs, so the day's spacing makes sense.
- business/staff.md — who's in, if there's a crew.
- templates/day-sheet.md — the shape the run sheet takes.

## Steps

1. Find tomorrow's book. If Claude can't see it, it stops and asks — it never fills
   a day from memory.
2. Walk the day in order. For each appointment: time, pet, parent, the groom, and
   about how long it should run.
3. Pull each pet's notes from clients.md — the usual cut, the blade length, "leave
   the ears fluffy", "takes a minute to settle". Only what's written or what you
   just said. A pet with no notes gets a blank, not a guess.
4. Flag the special asks: a first visit, a pet coming back after a long gap, a groom
   that tends to run long, back-to-back big dogs with no breather.
5. List the prep, only from what the book or your own notes point at — a blade worth
   checking before a thick double coat, or extra time blocked for a first-timer.
6. Show you the draft run sheet. You correct anything — a cancelled slot, a swapped
   time — and Claude fixes it from what you say.
7. Save it to reports/ so it's there with your coffee.

## What you get

One page saved as reports/day-sheet-YYYY-MM-DD.md:

- The day in order: time, pet, parent, groom, expected length.
- Coat and cut notes for each pet, straight from clients.md.
- Special asks and first visits, flagged plainly.
- A short prep list, and where the gaps in the day are.

## Never

- Never invent an appointment, a time, or a note. If the book and clients.md are
  both silent, the line stays blank with a question mark.
- Never put anything veterinary on the sheet — no medication notes, no sedation
  talk, no health instructions. A worry that needs the vet is written as exactly
  that: "worth mentioning to the parent, kindly".
- Never decide on the sheet how to handle a distressed, aggressive, elderly, or
  fragile pet — the sheet may flag it; your policy and your judgment decide it.
- Never send the run sheet anywhere or share it with anyone. It stays in reports/
  for you.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
