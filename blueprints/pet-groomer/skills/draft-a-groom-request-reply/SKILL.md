# Reply to a groom request

Turns a booking request into a friendly reply with the times you approved and the
intake questions a new pet needs, ready for you to send.

## When to run this

A pet parent is asking for a time. That might be a text you paste in, an Instagram
message, an email, or a note from a phone call. A reschedule counts too — treat the
new time like a fresh request and run the same steps.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — how long each groom takes, its usual price, and its due-back rhythm.
- business/staff.md — who grooms which days, if there's a crew.
- business/policies.md — deposits, cancellation rules, extra fees, and your hard limits.
- business/brand.md — your tone of voice, so the reply sounds like you.
- business/clients.md — if the pet is a regular, their usual groom and notes.
- templates/booking-reply.md — the shape the reply starts from.
- Your appointment book. Claude cannot see it on its own, so it asks you to paste
  the day or the week, or just tell it the open slots. No book, no time suggestions.

## Steps

1. Paste the request, or describe the call in your own words.
2. Claude repeats back what it heard: the pet, the groom they want, and when they'd
   like to come in. If a piece is missing, Claude asks you instead of guessing.
3. Claude looks up the groom in services.md for its length and usual price. If it is
   not listed there, Claude asks — it never makes a number up.
4. Claude asks which slots you want to offer, or works from the book you pasted.
   Only slots you named or confirmed go in the reply.
5. For a new pet, the reply folds in your intake questions, kept light: the breed,
   how the coat is doing and when it was last groomed, and anything the parent wants
   you to know about how their pet does with grooming. The parent volunteers; the
   reply never presses.
6. Prices go in as estimates, in plain words: the usual price for that groom, with a
   line saying the final price is your call once you've seen the coat.
7. Claude checks policies.md and folds in anything that applies — if a deposit is
   due or dematting costs extra, the reply says so now, not at pickup.
8. Claude shows you the draft and stops. You send it from your own phone or account,
   or ask for changes and Claude redrafts.
9. The parent picks a time and you write it in your book. Tell Claude once it is in —
   until then, Claude keeps calling the time "proposed", never "booked".
10. If something durable came up — a new regular, a coat note the parent offered —
    Claude shows you the exact line for clients.md and writes it only on your yes.

## What you get

- The reply, ready for you to copy and send yourself.
- A short note saved as reports/groom-request-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md holding the
  request, the slots offered, the estimate given, and the draft you approved. Handy
  when the parent replies two days later and you need the thread back.

## Never

- Never tells a parent a time is booked or held until you say it is written in your book.
- Never sends the reply itself — every message goes out from your hands.
- Never invents prices, hours, or open slots. If it is not in your files or in what
  you just said, Claude asks.
- Never quotes a firm price for a coat nobody has seen — estimates only, and the
  reply says plainly that the final price is yours to confirm.
- Never promises how a visit will go for a distressed, aggressive, elderly, or
  fragile pet — that decision is yours, by your policy, and the draft says you'll
  talk it through with the parent first.
- Never gives health or behavior advice — no diagnosis, no medication or sedation
  talk, no treatment claims. A worry about skin, ears, a lump, or temperament
  belongs with the vet, and the draft can say that kindly.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
