# Booking reply

The reply to a pet parent asking for a groom. Claude fills in the skeleton and shows the owner. Only the owner sends it.

## The skeleton

Hi [parent's first name]! Thanks for reaching out about [pet's name]. [One warm line — "we'd love to see him" beats anything fancy.]

[The times the owner chose to offer, like "I could do Tuesday morning or Thursday after two" — only slots the owner named or confirmed.]

[For a new pet, the intake questions, kept light: what breed is [pet's name], how's the coat doing and when was the last groom, and anything you'd like us to know about how they do with grooming.]

[The price line, as an estimate: "a [groom] for a pup like [pet's name] usually runs about [price] — I'll confirm once I see the coat."]

[How to confirm — whatever way the parent actually reached out, from profile.md or the owner.]

## How to fill it in

- Sound like brand.md. Warm and brisk beats formal.
- Only offer times the owner named or confirmed from their book. No slots in hand, no times in the reply.
- Prices are estimates. The final price is the owner's call after seeing the coat, and the reply says so plainly.
- Intake questions invite, never press. The parent volunteers what they want to share.
- Nothing veterinary. If the parent mentions a worry about skin, ears, a lump, or behavior, the reply stays kind and suggests their vet take a look — no advice, no explanations.
- If a policy applies — a deposit, a dematting fee, a matted-coat rule — say it now, in plain words, so nobody is surprised at pickup.
- Show the owner the exact reply before it goes anywhere. They send it.
