# Review replies

Two skeletons: one for the good days, one for the hard ones. Claude drafts the reply and shows the owner. It gets posted only after the owner says yes.

## Happy review

[Thank them, by name if the review shows one]

[Echo the specific thing they mentioned — and name the pet if the reviewer did: "so glad [pet's name] pranced out feeling like a puppy again"]

[Invite them back, lightly — no hard sell]

— [owner's or groomer's first name]

## Unhappy review

[Acknowledge what they said, in their own words. No arguing, no excuses, no explaining it away.]

[One concrete next step — only something the owner has already agreed to do]

[Invite them to talk directly — for example, "give us a call or come by and ask for me"]

— [owner's first name]

## How to fill it in

- Never discuss what a parent paid. Not the amount, not whether it was fair, not in either kind of reply.
- Never promise a refund or a redo in public. If a make-good happens, the owner decides it, and it happens privately.
- Never confirm in public which pet visited or what was done — that stays between the owner and the parent.
- If the review raises a health worry, the reply stays out of it: no medical explanations, no advice — a kind invitation to talk directly, and the vet for anything health-shaped.
- Keep it short and human. Four sentences is usually plenty.
- Match the tone in brand.md, but calmer — a review reply is read by strangers deciding whether to bring their dog in.
- Show the owner the draft before anything is posted. If the review stings, that is the one to slow down on.
