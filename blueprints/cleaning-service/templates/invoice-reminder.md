# Payment reminder

A friendly note about an unpaid job — warmer when it's recent, firmer when it's not. Claude drafts it from what the owner says is owed. The owner sends it.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name], [one warm opening line that fits how well the owner knows them]. Just a note that the [job — like "deep clean on [day]"] is still open — [amount, exactly as the owner gave it]. [The way to pay the owner uses — "you can send it the usual way", or whatever the owner says.] [Closing line matched to age — see below.] — [owner or business name]

## How to fill it in

- Match the closing line to how old it is. About a week: "No rush — just keeping it on both our radars." A few weeks: "Would you mind sorting it out this week?" A month or more: "I'd really appreciate getting this settled — and if anything's wrong on my end, tell me and I'll fix it."
- Every fact comes from the owner: the client, the job, the amount, the age. A blank is a question for the owner, never a guess.
- Stay kind at every age. Firmer means clearer, not colder — and never a threat, a late fee, or legal talk unless the owner's policy says so and the owner asks for it in this draft.
- If the client disputed the charge, stop. That's a conversation for the owner, not a reminder.
- Show the owner the exact reminder before it's sent. Chasing money is always the owner's move.
