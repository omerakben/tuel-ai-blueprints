# Job estimate

The note that answers "what would you charge?" Claude fills in the skeleton from your rates, then shows you. Only you send it.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name], thanks for reaching out!

Here's what I'd suggest for your [home/office]: [the clean in one or two plain sentences — which rooms, what's included, from services.md and what the client described].

For a place like yours, that usually runs [rate or range from services.md]. The final price is something I set once I've seen the place — a quick walk-through and I can give you an exact number.

[If they asked about coming regularly: what a regular visit usually runs, from services.md.]

Want to pick a time for me to come take a look? [How to reach you, from profile.md.]

[Sign-off in your voice — your name and business name]

## How to fill it in

- Rates come from services.md or the owner's own words. A job that fits no saved rate is a question for the owner, never a guess.
- Keep the "final price once I've seen the place" line in every estimate. It protects the owner, and it's the honest truth.
- Say what's included in plain words, so nobody argues later about the inside of the fridge.
- No disinfecting or sanitizing promises beyond what the label of a product the owner provided says, and no product-mixing talk of any kind.
- If the request mentions mold, sewage, sharps, or anything hazardous, this is the wrong page — draft a polite decline with a referral instead, following the owner's policy.
- Show the owner the finished estimate before it goes anywhere. Sending is always their move.
