# Recurring-clean offers

After a one-time clean that went well, draft the "want us back every two weeks?" note — ready for the owner to send while the place still smells fresh.

## When to run this

Right after a one-time job the owner enjoyed and would happily repeat. The same day is best, while the clean is fresh in the client's mind too.

## What Claude reads first

- business/clients.md — who the client is and anything already saved about them.
- business/services.md — what a regular visit usually runs, if it's saved.
- business/policies.md — anything the owner has said about regular clients.
- business/brand.md — how you talk.
- templates/recurring-offer.md — the shape the note starts from.

## Steps

1. Ask which client and how the job went. One line from the owner is plenty — "the townhouse today, it went great."
2. Check how to reach them. Only draft for a client who gave their number or asked to hear from the owner. If that's not clear, ask.
3. Check services.md for a regular-visit rate. If there isn't one, ask the owner what they'd offer — a rhythm, a rate, or both. Never invent either.
4. Draft the note from templates/recurring-offer.md: short, warm, specific to the job just done, with an easy way to say no thanks.
5. Show the owner the draft and wait. They edit it or okay it, then send it from their own phone.
6. Save a copy to reports/ so the owner can see later who was offered what.
7. If the client says yes, propose the update to business/clients.md — their rhythm and their day — and wait for the owner's yes before writing it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/recurring-offer-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The client and the job the offer came from.
- The note, ready to copy and send.
- A line for what the client said, so the answer has somewhere to land.

## Never

- Never send the note, and never follow up on it unless the owner asks.
- Never invent a rate or a discount. The numbers come from services.md or from the owner, nowhere else.
- Never pressure. One warm offer with an easy out — if the client says no or says nothing, that's the end of it unless the owner says otherwise.
- Never make disinfecting or sanitizing claims beyond what the label of a product the owner provided says.
- Never draft for a client who asked not to be contacted.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
