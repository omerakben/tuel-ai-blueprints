# Route planning

Propose next week's route order from your client list and your notes, so the driving is short and the days make sense. A proposal only — you set the calendar.

## When to run this

Toward the end of the week, once the owner knows who's on for next week. It's the heart of the weekly routes hour (workflows/weekly-routes.md), and it works fine on its own too.

## What Claude reads first

- business/clients.md — the regulars, how often they like you to come, the day they prefer, and the part of town they're in.
- business/staff.md — who works which days, if there's a crew.
- business/policies.md — anything about working hours or days off.
- What the owner says about next week — one-time jobs, cancellations, a client who asked to move.

## Steps

1. Gather next week's jobs. Start from the regulars who are due, then add whatever the owner says is booked. If a regular's rhythm or day isn't saved and the owner hasn't said, ask — never assume someone is on the list.
2. Group the jobs by day, keeping every saved preference. A client whose page says Tuesdays stays on Tuesday unless the owner moves them.
3. Order each day by part of town, so one job leads to the next without crossing the map. Note the reasoning in one line per day — "both on the east side, so back to back."
4. Flag the tight spots as questions: a day too full, two jobs far apart, a job with no saved duration.
5. Show the owner the whole week as a proposal. They move anything, cut anything, add anything. Nothing is settled until they say so.
6. If a client would need to move, draft the "would Thursday work instead?" text for the owner to send. The route plan never changes a client's day by itself — only the owner's sent message and the client's yes do that.
7. Save the plan to reports/ once the owner is happy with it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/route-plan-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The week at a glance: each day's jobs in driving order, with the one-line reasoning.
- The questions — anything that needs the owner's call before the week starts.
- Any drafted "can we move you?" texts, waiting for the owner to send.

## Never

- Never treat the plan as booked. It's a proposal until the owner says otherwise, and a client only moves when the owner tells them and they agree.
- Never guess who's due. If the rhythm isn't in clients.md and the owner hasn't said, it's a question in the plan, not a job on the list.
- Never put a door code, alarm code, key location, or entry instruction in the plan. "Owner handles getting in" is as specific as a plan ever gets.
- Never message anyone. Every drafted text waits in the report for the owner.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
