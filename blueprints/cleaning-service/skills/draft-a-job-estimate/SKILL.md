# Job estimates

Turn "what would you charge?" into a clear estimate drafted from your own rates, ready to send — with the final price always yours to set after you've seen the place.

## When to run this

The moment an inquiry lands — a call, a text, a message from the website. The goal is an estimate the owner can send back in minutes, not an evening.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — your rates, and how you price jobs you haven't seen.
- business/policies.md — what a standard clean includes, and the jobs you turn down.
- business/profile.md — where you work and when.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the estimate sounds like you.
- templates/estimate.md — the shape the estimate starts from.

## Steps

1. Get the picture of the job. Home or office, how many rooms and bathrooms, the shape it's in ("hasn't been cleaned in a while" changes the number), one-time or a regular rhythm, and any extras like the inside of the oven or the windows. If the size or the scope is missing, stop and ask — there is no estimate without them.
2. Check the job against the turn-down list in policies.md. If it involves mold, sewage, sharps, or anything hazardous, skip the estimate: draft a polite no instead, pointing to a specialist if the owner has named one. That draft goes to the owner for approval like any other.
3. Build the numbers from services.md only. If the job doesn't fit a saved rate, ask the owner what they'd charge — never bridge the gap with a guess.
4. Draft the estimate from templates/estimate.md, in the voice from brand.md. It must say plainly that the final price is the owner's call after seeing the place.
5. Show the owner the draft and wait. They edit it or say yes, and they send it themselves.
6. Save the estimate to reports/ so there's a record of what was offered.
7. If the inquiry taught you something durable — a new client's name, the part of town they're in — propose the lines for business/clients.md and wait for a yes before writing.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/job-estimate-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- What the client asked for, in one short paragraph.
- The estimate, ready to copy and send.
- Anything still unknown, listed as questions for the walk-through.

## Never

- Never invent a rate. If services.md is silent and the owner hasn't said, ask.
- Never promise a firm price for a place nobody has seen. Every estimate says the final price comes after the owner looks.
- Never make disinfecting or sanitizing claims beyond what the label of a product the owner provided says, and never give advice about mixing products — the label is the whole answer.
- Never estimate mold, asbestos, lead, sewage, sharps, or biohazard work. Those get a polite decline and a referral, per the owner's policy — never advice.
- Never write down a door code, alarm code, key location, or entry instruction, even if the client offers one in their message. Say so and leave it out.
- Never send the estimate. The owner reads it, okays it, and sends it.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
