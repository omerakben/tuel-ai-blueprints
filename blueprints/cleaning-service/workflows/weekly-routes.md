# Weekly routes hour

One hour a week to set next week up: who's due, which day, what order. Claude proposes, you decide.

## When

Once a week, near the end of the week — Thursday or Friday afternoon, whenever the owner knows who's on for next week. Say "let's plan the routes" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads this week's route plan in reports/ and asks what actually happened — jobs that ran long, a client who moved, a day that felt wrong. If there's no plan yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Gather next week. Claude runs the route-planning playbook (skills/plan-the-routes/): regulars who are due from business/clients.md, plus everything the owner adds — new jobs, cancellations, a week off. Anyone whose rhythm isn't saved and hasn't been named becomes a question, not a booking.
3. Propose the week. Each day's jobs in driving order, one line of reasoning per day, every tight spot flagged as a question.
4. Adjust together. The owner moves, cuts, and adds until the week looks right. Nothing is settled until the owner says so.
5. Draft the asks. If a client would need to move, Claude drafts the "would Thursday work instead?" text — each one waits for the owner to send it from their own phone.
6. Save the plan. With the owner's yes, the plan and any drafted texts go to reports/ as one file, ready for the morning briefs to read all week.

## What you get

- Next week at a glance, saved as reports/weekly-routes-[date].md: each day in driving order, the reasoning, the open questions.
- Any "can we move you?" texts, drafted and waiting for the owner.

## Never

- Claude never books, moves, or confirms anything with a client. The plan is a proposal; only the owner's sent message and the client's answer change a booking.
- Claude never guesses who's due, how long a job takes, or what day a client prefers. Missing facts show up as questions in the plan.
- No door codes, alarm codes, key locations, or entry instructions in the plan, ever.
- A yes to the plan covers the plan. Every text drafted from it still needs its own fresh yes before the owner sends it.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
