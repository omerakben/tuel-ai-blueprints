- Confirm the local time zone and usual working days.
- List which files and connected tools Claude may read for routine work.

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Truck name
- What the truck serves, and the feel of it in three words
- Usual service days and hours
- Just the owner, or a crew — names, what each person handles, which days (just you? that's fine)
- Top menu items with price and a one-line description in the owner's words
- The regular spots, and how each one gets confirmed: whose permission, what permit, what hours
- Where the owner announces the day's location today

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full menu with prices (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- Par levels: how much of each staple the owner likes to start a service with
- Suppliers: who supplies the food, and who supplies the rest (paper goods, propane) — and how orders get placed
- Event and catering basics: what the truck offers, and what the owner needs to know before saying yes (date, crowd size, power, space)
- House policies: event deposits, cancellations, rain days, refunds at the window
- Logo and a few photos the owner likes, dropped into `assets/`
- The truck's look and voice: three words, colors, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, Yelp) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- Restock points: how low an item gets before it's time to order more
- The commissary or home base, and its rhythm
- Busiest and slowest spots and days, as the owner sees them
- The truck's story — how it started, what it's known for
- How each crew member takes their pay, only if the owner wants the daily close to track it
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (permits, insurance, inspections)
