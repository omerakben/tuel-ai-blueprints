# Event inquiry reply

Turns a pasted event or booking inquiry into a warm reply that gathers the details you need — date, crowd size, power, space — without committing you to anything.

## When to run this

Someone asked about booking the truck: a wedding, an office lunch, a brewery night, a festival. Paste in the email, the message, or your notes from the call, and Claude drafts the reply.

A follow-up counts too. If an organizer answers your questions, run this again with their reply to draft the next message.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — what you offer for events and what you need to know before saying yes, if you've filled that part in.
- business/policies.md — your deposit, cancellation, and weather rules, so the reply matches how you actually work.
- business/profile.md — the truck's name and how people reach you.
- business/brand.md — your voice, so the reply sounds like you.
- templates/event-inquiry-reply.md — the shape the reply starts from.

From you: the inquiry itself, pasted or described in your own words.

## Steps

1. Paste the inquiry. Claude repeats back what it heard: who's asking, what kind of event, and any details they already gave.
2. Claude lists what's known and what's missing from the things you need before saying yes — the date, the times, the place, the crowd size, whether there's power, and whether there's room for the truck. If services.md has your own list, that list wins.
3. Claude drafts the reply from templates/event-inquiry-reply.md: a warm thank-you, the questions that are still open, and what happens next. Facts about the truck come from business/ only.
4. If they asked about price, the draft says you'll follow up with numbers once you've seen the details — unless you or business/policies.md give an amount to share right now. Anything shared is called an estimate, and the reply says plainly that the final price is your call after you've seen the whole picture.
5. Show you the draft and wait. You edit or approve, then send it yourself from your own email or account.
6. Save the exchange to reports/ so the thread is easy to pick back up when they answer.
7. If the organizer or their event is worth remembering, Claude offers one line for business/clients.md and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

One file in reports/, named like reports/event-inquiry-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- What the inquiry asked for, in two or three lines.
- The reply you approved, ready to copy and send.
- The open questions you're waiting on, so the next chat starts warm.

## Never

- Never accept, decline, hold a date, or promise the truck will be anywhere. Every commitment is the owner's, made fresh.
- Never give a price the owner didn't set. Estimates only, clearly called estimates, and the final price is the owner's call after seeing the whole job.
- Never claim the truck can handle a site's power, space, or permit situation. Those are questions for the organizer and calls for the owner — permits are the owner's alone.
- Never make allergen, cross-contact, or sourcing claims beyond the exact words in business/services.md.
- Never send the reply. The owner sends every message from their own account.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
