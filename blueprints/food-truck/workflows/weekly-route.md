# Weekly route hour

One hour a week to plan where the truck goes next week — Claude builds the route sheet and drafts the follow-ups, and you confirm every spot.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. Many owners use the day after their busiest service, when the week's rhythm is fresh. Say "let's plan the route" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's route sheet and the week's daily closes in reports/: which spots pulled, which dragged, what sold out where. If there is no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Lay out next week. Day by day, you name the spot you're aiming for — a regular corner, an event, a day off. Claude only works from spots you name; it never proposes a location as if it were available.
3. Mark what's confirmed. For each day, Claude asks the one question that matters: is this spot squared away — permission, permit, and hours? A yes makes it confirmed. Anything else is penciled in, and the sheet says so plainly.
4. Draft the follow-ups. For every penciled spot, Claude drafts the message that would confirm it — a note to the lot owner, a reply to the event organizer — for you to send. Sending is yours, and so is every deal made in those messages.
5. Line up the posts. For confirmed days, Claude notes that the evening helper in schedules/tomorrows-location-post.md will draft each location post the night before. Nothing is written ahead for penciled days — a post waits for a confirmed spot.
6. Review the sheet. Claude shows you the whole week: each day's spot, hours, confirmed or penciled, and what each penciled day is waiting on. You correct anything, then give the go-ahead.
7. Save it. With your yes, Claude saves the sheet as reports/weekly-route-[date].md. The morning brief and the evening post check read from it all week.

## What you get

- One route sheet for the week, saved as reports/weekly-route-[date].md: each day's spot, hours, and status, with penciled days clearly marked and what they're waiting on.
- Drafted confirmation messages for the penciled spots, ready to copy once you've approved each one.

## Never

- Claude never marks a spot confirmed. Only your word does that — permission, permit, and hours, all three.
- Claude never sends a message to a lot owner, an organizer, or anyone else. You send every one yourself.
- Claude never reads a pattern as a plan. Three Tuesdays at the same corner make a habit, not a booking.
- Parking rules, permit questions, and anything a health inspector cares about are yours alone — Claude can hold a date you give it, never an opinion on what's allowed.
- A penciled spot never appears in a post, a reply, or anything a customer might see.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
