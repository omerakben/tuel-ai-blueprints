# What to collect, in order

<!-- TUEL:CONTROL-CHECKLIST:START -->
## Control choices

- Name the person who approves decisions and outside actions.
- Confirm the local time zone and usual working days.
- List which files and connected tools Claude may read for routine work.
- Choose how long temporary exports in `inbox/` should be kept. Claude may flag old files but never deletes them.
- Name the accountant, lawyer, technician, or other qualified person who reviews work outside Claude's lane, when relevant.
<!-- TUEL:CONTROL-CHECKLIST:END -->

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Studio name
- The kind of work (custom, flash, cover-ups, a mix) and the feel of the place in three words
- Days and hours, and whether walk-ins happen or it's appointment only
- Who tattoos here — maybe just the owner — with what each artist is known for and which days they're in
- How people get in touch when they want work
- How booking goes once someone's serious: consult first, deposit to hold a date — in the owner's words
- The age and ID rule, in the owner's words
- Where the waitlist lives, if there is one

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- How pricing is framed — by the hour, by the piece, a day rate — in words, since the final price of any piece is always the owner's call after seeing the idea
- House policies: deposits, cancellations, no-shows, touch-ups
- The aftercare sheet: a photo or file dropped into `assets/`, so Claude can quote it word for word instead of ever improvising
- Ongoing projects: pieces in progress, who's mid-sleeve, who's healing, who's waiting on a drawing
- Suppliers: ink, needles and cartridges, gloves, aftercare product — and how orders get placed
- Logo and a few photos the owner likes, dropped into `assets/`, with a note of which clients said yes to sharing
- The studio's look and voice: three words, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, Yelp) and whether the owner wants drafted replies

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- Reorder points: how low supplies get before it's time to order
- Busiest and slowest stretches of the year, as the owner sees them
- The studio's story — how it started, what it's known for
- Guest artists and conventions, if they're part of the year
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (studio license, artist registrations, insurance)
