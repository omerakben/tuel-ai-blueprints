# Weekly bookings hour

One hour a week to get the book in shape — Claude preps the lists and the drafts, and you make every call.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. Many owners use the quiet stretch before the first appointment. Say "let's do the bookings hour" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's bookings-hour report in reports/ and checks what went out, who replied, and what's still open. If there's no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Lay out the week. Paste this week's book or point to a copy in assets/. Claude sets it out plainly: sessions, consults, the gaps, and anything that looks double-booked — raised as a question, never a correction.
3. Prep the consults. For each consult this week, Claude checks reports/ for a consult brief and offers to prepare any that are missing using skills/prepare-a-consult-brief/, one at a time.
4. Clear the inquiry pile. Paste anything unanswered and Claude drafts replies with skills/draft-an-inquiry-reply/ — each one shown to you before it goes anywhere.
5. Fill the gaps. If the week looks thin, Claude offers waitlist outreach using skills/prepare-waitlist-outreach/: the who-to-contact list first, drafts only after your go-ahead.
6. Chase the loose ends. Deposits you say are still unpaid go on a plain follow-up list with a drafted nudge for each — you decide which nudges to send. Claude never checks a balance on its own and never treats a deposit as paid or unpaid without your word.
7. Wrap up. Claude saves the hour to reports/: the week at a glance, every approved draft, and what's waiting on you.

## What you get

- One page saved as reports/weekly-bookings-[date].md: the week's sessions and consults, the gaps, and the follow-up list.
- The approved drafts underneath — replies, notes, and nudges, ready to copy and send.

## Never

- Never book, move, or cancel an appointment or a consult. The book is yours; Claude works from the copy you share.
- Never send a reply, a note, or a nudge. Every draft waits for your fresh yes, and you do the sending.
- Never invent a booking, a gap, a deposit, or a waitlist entry. What you share is all there is.
- A yes covers that exact item, this week. Last week's yes approves nothing now, and silence on an item is a no.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
