# Draft an inquiry reply

Turns a message from someone who wants work into a reply that gathers what the artist
needs — the idea, rough size, placement, and reference pictures — while pricing and
dates stay where they belong: with you.

## When to run this

- A booking inquiry just landed: an Instagram message, an email, a note from a call,
  or a form someone filled in. Paste it here.
- A half-answered thread needs a next reply. Paste the back-and-forth and pick up
  where it left off.
- This is also the first-win playbook from your first chat — it works the same on
  day one and day one hundred.

## What Claude reads first

- business/profile.md — how the studio works: hours, walk-ins or appointment only,
  how people usually book.
- business/policies.md — the age and ID rule, deposits, and how consults happen here.
- business/services.md — what the studio offers, so the reply never promises work
  the studio doesn't do.
- business/brand.md — your voice, so the reply sounds like you.
- templates/inquiry-reply.md — the shape the draft starts from.

## Steps

1. Paste the inquiry, exact words and all. Claude repeats back what it heard: the
   idea, anything about size or placement, and any questions they asked. If part of
   it is unclear, Claude asks you — never a guess.
2. Claude checks the inquiry against the age and ID rule in business/policies.md.
   If the message raises age at all, the draft states your rule plainly and kindly —
   never a workaround, never an exception. If your rule isn't written down yet,
   Claude stops and asks you for it before drafting a word.
3. Claude drafts the reply from templates/inquiry-reply.md, in your voice: warm,
   short, and asking for whatever is still missing — the idea in their words, rough
   size in everyday terms, placement, and reference pictures.
4. The draft asks them to send reference pictures. When those arrive, drop them into
   assets/ under the client's first name — that's where the consult brief will look
   for them later.
5. No prices and no dates go in the draft. It says you price every piece after
   seeing the idea and will come back on timing. If you want a price or a date in
   this exact reply, say it and Claude writes exactly that.
6. Claude shows you the draft and stops. You send it from your own account, or ask
   for changes and look again.
7. Claude saves the exchange to reports/ so the thread is easy to pick back up when
   they answer.
8. If something durable came up — a name, an idea worth tracking — Claude offers one
   line for business/clients.md and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

One file per inquiry, saved as reports/inquiry-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the original
message, the reply you approved, and what's still missing (size not given yet,
references not in assets/ yet). When the client answers, paste their reply into the
same chat and Claude picks up the thread.

## Never

- Never put a price, an estimate, or a date in a reply unless you gave it for this
  exact reply. Pricing and dates are always the artist's call.
- Never draft anything that works around an age, ID, or guardian-consent rule — the
  rule gets stated plainly, or the message goes to you. Licensing questions go to
  you as well.
- Never comment on skin, healing, allergies, or anything medical. If they ask about
  aftercare, the studio's own printed sheet in assets/ is quoted word for word — no
  sheet on file means no aftercare talk.
- Never send the reply itself, or set one up to send later. Every message leaves by
  your hand.
- Never invent what the studio offers. If it isn't in business/ or something you
  just said, Claude asks.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
