# Prepare a consult brief

One calm page before a consult: the idea in the client's own words, their references,
placement, and size — so you walk in ready. No design decisions live here; that's what
the consult is for.

## When to run this

- A consult is on the book for today or tomorrow.
- The morning brief flagged a consult that has no brief yet.
- You say "get me ready for [first name]" any time before they arrive.

## What Claude reads first

- The inquiry thread — the saved reports/inquiry-reply files for this client, or the
  messages you paste in.
- assets/ — the reference pictures the client sent, saved under their first name.
- business/clients.md — anything already remembered about this client and their piece.
- templates/consult-brief.md — the one-page shape.

## Steps

1. Confirm who the consult is for and when. If two clients share a first name, Claude
   asks which one rather than assuming.
2. Gather what's known. The idea goes in the client's exact words — quoted, not
   polished into art direction. Placement and size stay as they said them, everyday
   terms and all.
3. List the references by file name from assets/. If nothing is there yet, the brief
   says "references: none in the folder yet" — Claude never describes a picture it
   hasn't seen.
4. Anything unknown becomes a question on the "still missing" list, for you to ask at
   the consult. A blank is a question, never a guess.
5. If the client mentioned anything health-related, the brief carries one line only —
   "raised a health question, one for you at the consult" — and the details stay out
   of every file.
6. If a reference looks like another artist's finished tattoo, the brief says so
   plainly. That's useful for the conversation about making the piece their own, and
   the design is never treated as original when it isn't.
7. Claude shows you the draft brief. You adjust or approve, and it's saved to reports/.

## What you get

One page saved as reports/consult-brief-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the idea in the client's words,
placement, size, the reference list, their open questions, and what's still missing.
Short enough to read while the coffee's still hot.

## Never

- Never make a design decision — no style suggestions, no "this would sit better on
  the shoulder", no picking between references. The brief carries facts and questions;
  you decide at the consult.
- Never write health details into the brief or into business/ — a health question is
  flagged in one line with no specifics.
- Never describe references that aren't in assets/, and never invent one.
- Never put a price or a date in the brief unless you already set one for this client.
- Never work around age, ID, or guardian-consent rules. If the thread leaves any doubt
  about age, that flag goes at the top of the brief — the check at the door is yours,
  and no draft softens it.
