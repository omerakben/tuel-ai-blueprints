# Weekly rhythm hour

One sitting a week to line up the whole week: the job order, the shopping list, and the nudges — Claude drafts, you decide.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. Many owners use Sunday evening or Monday's first coffee. Say "let's plan the week" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's plan and the end-of-day notes in reports/: what got done, what slid, who's still owed an answer. If there's no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Gather this week's list. You tell Claude every job on your plate — new calls, holdovers, the thing you keep putting off. Claude asks for the plain basics on anything new, and asks rather than guesses.
3. Set the order. Claude proposes the week using the plan-the-week playbook (skills/plan-the-week/): jobs grouped by area, promised days first, not-ready jobs parked with a note. You rearrange until it's your week — it stays a proposal until you call it the plan.
4. Build the shopping list. Claude turns the settled jobs into one list sorted by store, using the materials playbook (skills/prepare-a-materials-list/). Safety-critical picks stay yours.
5. Sweep the quiet estimates. Claude drafts a friendly nudge for each estimate still unanswered, using the follow-up playbook (skills/draft-an-estimate-follow-up/). You approve each one by name.
6. Sweep the unpaid invoices. Same shape: you give the list, Claude drafts the reminders (skills/draft-an-invoice-reminder/), and you approve each one by name.
7. Wrap it up. Everything lands in reports/ in one bundle: the week plan, the shopping list, and every approved draft, ready for you to send in your own time.

## What you get

- The week's plan, with the reason for each placement, saved as reports/weekly-jobs-[date].md.
- The shopping list by store, and every approved nudge and reminder, underneath it in the same file.

## Never

- Claude never sends, books, buys, or pays anything in this hour. Every draft waits for your hands.
- Claude never invents a job, a rate, a date, or an amount. If it's not in business/, in reports/, or something you just said, it asks.
- A yes covers that exact item, this week. Last week's yes approves nothing today, and staying quiet on an item is a no.
- See-it-first work (electrical, gas, structural, roofing, asbestos, lead, mold) goes on the plan as a look-first visit, never as a priced job ready to go.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
