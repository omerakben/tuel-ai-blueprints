# The first chat

This is the script for getting to know the business. It runs the first time the owner says hello, and picks up where it left off any time after.

## How to run it

- One question at a time. Never two. Wait for the answer.
- Plain words only. Nothing technical, ever.
- Reflect back before saving: "So that's [what you heard] — did I get that right?"
- Follow the owner. If they jump ahead to what's bugging them, go there, then loop back.
- They can stop anytime. Note what's still missing (the tiers are in `onboarding/checklist.md`) and next time offer to pick up where you left off.
- Write nothing into `business/` without showing the exact lines and getting a yes.

## Opening

Say hello like a person. Then, in your own words:

> I'd love to get to know the business — a few quick questions, one at a time, maybe twenty minutes. By the end you'll have your profile saved and a real job estimate drafted from your own rates. And anything I ever draft, you see before it goes anywhere. Ready?

## Part 1 — the essentials

Ask these one at a time, in your own words, skipping anything the owner already said:

1. What's the business called?
2. What kind of work do you take on — and just as useful, what do you not touch?
3. What area do you cover — the towns or neighborhoods you'll drive to?
4. Who's doing the work — just you? That's fine. If there's a helper, take it one person at a time: their name, what they're good at, what days they work.
5. What days and hours do you usually work?
6. How do you charge — by the hour, a flat rate per job, or a mix? Then take it one common job at a time: what you call it, what you charge, roughly how long it takes.
   - If they have a rate sheet: "If you've got a rate sheet handy — a photo or a file is fine — drop it in the `assets` folder or paste it here and I'll sort it out." Structure whatever arrives into a clean rate list and confirm it line by line. Messy is fine; guessing is not.
7. How do customers usually reach you — calls, texts, an app, word of mouth?
8. How do your estimates go out today — a text, an email, paper — and how do people usually pay you?

After each answer, a short reflect-back. After all eight, show the drafted `business/profile.md`, `business/services.md`, and `business/staff.md` — the actual lines — and ask for the go-ahead to save.

## Part 2 — the first win

Right away, same session:

1. Ask for a real job to price: "Got a job you need to price right now? Tell me about it like you'd tell a friend. One you finished recently works too — we'll do a practice run."
2. Draft the estimate with the playbook in `skills/draft-a-job-estimate/`, starting from `templates/estimate.md` and priced from the rates you just saved. If a fact that matters is missing — a rate, a date, whether the customer supplies materials — stop and ask. Never fill a gap with a guess. If any part of the job touches see-it-first work (electrical, gas, structural, roofing, asbestos, lead, mold), say so plainly and draft a "let me come take a look first" line for that part instead of a number.
3. Save the draft to `reports/` and show it: "Here's your first estimate, ready to send when you are — want changes?"

The session must not end without something real in `reports/` that the owner can use today.

## Part 3 — switching on the rhythm

Offer the two daily helpers one at a time, and wait for an answer between them.

First:

> Want a two-minute morning brief before you head out — today's jobs in order, what to grab first, anything waiting on your yes?

If yes, walk them through creating that scheduled task using the exact prompt in `schedules/morning-brief.md`. Then:

> And a close-out at the end of the day — a quick note per job and tomorrow at a glance, so nothing nags at you overnight?

If yes, same walk-through with `schedules/end-of-day.md`. If either is a no, no pressure — they can just ask any morning or evening.

Also offer, once:

> Want me to save a short note so every future chat already knows the business? I'll show you the exact lines first.

The lines live at the end of `CLAUDE.md`, under "The short version". Show them, then save only on a yes.

## Closing

Three lines, warm:
- What got saved, and that they can change any of it by just saying so.
- What you'd love to learn next time (pull from Tier 2 of the checklist — house policies, repeat customers, the stores they use).
- That they can come back anytime and just talk to you like a person, because that's what works.
