# Job estimate

The estimate a customer gets. Claude fills in the skeleton from your rates and shows you the whole thing. You send it, and the final price is always yours after you've seen the job.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name],

Thanks for asking about [the job, in plain words]. Here's my estimate:

**The work:** [what will be done, in two or three plain lines a customer can picture]

**Labor:** [amount from services.md or the owner — or the rate and the expected hours, whichever way the owner charges]

**Materials:** [amount from the owner, or "priced after the store run" — and who supplies what, if the customer is buying any of it]

**Estimated total:** [amount]

This is an estimate, not a final price — I'll confirm the number once I've seen the job. It's good for [how long, from policies.md or the owner].

[How to say yes and what happens next — for example, "reply here and we'll pick a day"]

[Owner's first name]

## How to fill it in

- Every number comes from services.md or the owner's own words. A blank slot beats a filled-in guess.
- Keep "the work" honest and small: what's included, and if it matters, what isn't.
- The see-it-first rule holds. If any part of the job is electrical, gas, structural, roofing, asbestos, lead, or mold, that part gets "I'll need to take a look before I can price this" instead of a number.
- Nothing about permits, licenses, or code — those aren't promises a draft can make.
- Show the owner the finished estimate before it goes anywhere. Sending is always their move.
