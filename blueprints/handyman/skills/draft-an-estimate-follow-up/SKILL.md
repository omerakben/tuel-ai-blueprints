# Follow up on quiet estimates

Estimates that went out and got no answer deserve one friendly nudge. Claude drafts the nudges; you decide who hears from you, and you send every one.

## When to run this

- The weekly estimate sweep flagged a few gone quiet (schedules/estimate-chase.md does this for you).
- You just remembered a job you priced two weeks ago and never heard back on.
- The week ahead looks light and there's work sitting in unanswered estimates.

## What Claude reads first

- reports/ — the saved estimates, so every nudge says what was actually offered.
- business/clients.md — how each customer likes to be reached, and any "don't chase" notes.
- business/policies.md — how long your estimates stay good, and any limits you've set on reaching out.
- business/brand.md — your voice, so a nudge sounds like you, not like a bill collector.
- templates/follow-up-note.md — the shape each nudge starts from.

## Steps

1. Build the quiet list. Claude gathers the estimates in reports/ that went out and show no reply. If it can't tell whether one was answered, it asks you — never assumes.
2. Show you the list before writing a word. You cross off anyone — the customer who said "maybe in spring", the neighbor you'd rather not press. Only names you keep get drafts.
3. Draft one nudge per kept name from templates/follow-up-note.md: short, warm, and specific to their estimate. No two read the same, and none of them pushes.
4. Show you the whole batch. You edit or cut any draft, then approve the keepers by name. You send them yourself, your way.
5. Save the batch to reports/ so next week's sweep knows who already got a nudge.
6. If an answer changes something durable — "they sold the house", "text only, never call" — Claude proposes the line for clients.md and waits for your yes before writing it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/estimate-follow-ups-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The quiet list: customer, job, and when the estimate went out.
- One draft nudge per approved name, ready to copy and send.
- The skipped list, with the reason each was skipped.

## Never

- Never send a nudge, and never set one up to go out later. Your hands, your phone, every time.
- Never nudge anyone who said no or asked for space, and never nudge twice without telling you about the first one.
- Never sweeten the deal to close it. A discount or a price change is your call, made fresh — Claude won't put one in a draft on its own.
- Never restate the estimate from memory. Numbers come from the saved copy in reports/, or Claude asks you.
- Never turn a nudge into advice about the job itself. If the customer asked a question about see-it-first work (electrical, gas, structural, roofing, asbestos, lead, mold), the draft offers a visit, not an answer.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
