# Draft a job estimate

Tell Claude about the job the way you'd tell a friend, and get back an estimate priced from your own rates — ready for you to send, with the final number always yours after you've seen the job.

## When to run this

- A customer just called or texted about a job and you want a number back to them today.
- You're pricing a bigger job and want the pieces laid out before you commit to anything.
- You're between jobs and want the estimate drafted by the time you park the truck.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — your rates and how you charge. Every number starts here.
- business/policies.md — deposits, payment terms, and how long your estimates stay good.
- business/clients.md — past jobs for this customer, if they're a repeat.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the estimate reads like you wrote it.
- templates/estimate.md — the shape every estimate starts from.

## Steps

1. Get the job in plain words: what needs doing, where, what the place is like, and who's supplying materials. If the scope is fuzzy — "some stuff in the bathroom" — Claude stops and asks until it can picture the job. No picture, no estimate.
2. Check the lane. If any part of the job touches electrical, gas, structural, roofing, asbestos, lead, or mold, Claude says so and drafts a "let me come take a look first" line for that part instead of a number. See-it-first work never gets priced sight unseen.
3. Price the labor from services.md. If the job isn't on your rate list, or nothing there truly fits, Claude asks what you'd charge — it never stretches a rate to cover a job it wasn't made for.
4. List the materials, priced only from what you say. If you don't know a price yet, the line reads [materials — priced after the store run], never a guess.
5. Build the draft from templates/estimate.md, including the line that matters: this is an estimate, and the final price is confirmed once you've seen the job.
6. Show you the draft and wait. You change anything you like; Claude redrafts until it's right. Then you send it — from your phone or your email, your way.
7. Save it to reports/ so the weekly estimate sweep can find it later.
8. If the chat surfaced something durable — a rate worth adding to services.md, a note about a repeat customer — Claude shows you the exact line and waits for your yes before writing it.

## What you get

One file in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/job-estimate-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The estimate, ready to copy and send.
- The scope in plain words, as you described it.
- Open questions — anything to confirm on site before the final price.
- A "sent?" line so you can mark when it went out.

## Never

- Never a number that didn't come from services.md or your own mouth. No guessed rates, no guessed material prices, no padding.
- Never call it a quote or a final price. It's an estimate, and the final number is yours after you've seen the job.
- Never price, explain, or reassure about electrical, gas, structural, roofing, asbestos, lead, or mold work from here — that's see-it-first, always.
- Never claim the work is permitted, licensed, or up to code.
- Never send the estimate. It waits in reports/ until you send it yourself.
- Never keep a door code, an alarm code, or a key location if one comes up while describing the job — Claude says so and leaves it out.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
