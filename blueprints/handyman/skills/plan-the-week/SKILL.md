# Plan the week

Hand Claude your list of jobs and get back a proposed order for the week — a proposal you rearrange, never a plan decided for you.

## When to run this

- Sunday evening or Monday morning, coffee in hand, before the week starts pulling.
- Mid-week, when a cancellation or a rain day scrambles what you had.
- Any time the list in your head gets longer than you like.

## What Claude reads first

- Your job list for the week. Paste it, read it out, or point at last week's plan in reports/ and say what changed.
- business/profile.md — your working days, hours, and the area you cover.
- business/staff.md — who's available which days, if it's not just you.
- business/clients.md — notes that affect timing, like a customer who's only home mornings.
- business/policies.md — anything you've promised about how fast you get back to people.

## Steps

1. Gather the list. For each job Claude needs the plain basics: what it is, roughly where, about how long, and whether anything was promised. Missing pieces become questions, not guesses.
2. Set aside the not-ready jobs. Anything waiting on a part, a material, or a see-it-first visit gets parked with a note — not slotted in as if it were ready to go.
3. Propose an order, with reasons you can check: jobs in the same part of town grouped together, the long job given a clear run, promised days honored first. Every reason sits next to its job.
4. Show you the proposal and wait. You move things in plain words — "swap Tuesday and Thursday" — and Claude redoes the plan until it looks like your week.
5. If you want customers told, Claude drafts a short "does [day] suit you?" note for each — drafts only, and you send every one yourself.
6. Save the plan to reports/ so the morning brief and the shopping list can work from it.

## What you get

One page in reports/, a fresh one each run, named like reports/week-plan-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Inside:

- The week, day by day, with the reason each job sits where it does.
- The not-ready list — jobs waiting on parts, materials, or a look in person.
- Any drafted "does this day work?" notes, waiting for your yes.

## Never

- This is a proposal, never a booking. No date is promised to anyone until you say it to them yourself.
- Never invent how long a job takes or when a customer is free. If it's not in business/, in the list you gave, or something you just said, Claude asks.
- Never message a customer. Date-check notes are drafts; you send them.
- Never slot in see-it-first work (electrical, gas, structural, roofing, asbestos, lead, mold) as if it were priced and ready — it goes on the plan as a look-first visit instead.
- Never treat a helper's day off as open time, and never promise a helper's hours without the owner saying so.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
