# Draft a custom order reply

Turns a cake or custom bake inquiry into a warm reply that gathers the details and sums up the order so far — the price, the date, and the yes stay yours.

## When to run this

Someone is asking about a custom order: a birthday cake in your messages, an email about a celebration bake, a note scribbled at the counter. Paste it in or tell Claude about it, in any shape.

A follow-up counts too. When the customer answers your questions, run this again to update the summary and draft the next reply.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — the custom bakes you take, any starting prices (estimates, never quotes), and the notice you like.
- business/policies.md — deposits, cancellations, and pickup rules, so the reply matches your house rules.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, plus the exact allergen words you use, if you've saved any.
- business/clients.md — in case it's a regular with a usual.
- templates/custom-order-form.md — the details every order needs before you can say yes.

## Steps

1. Paste the inquiry, or describe the call or counter chat in your own words. Claude repeats back what it heard: who's asking, what bake, and any details already given. If a piece is unclear, Claude asks you — it never guesses.
2. Claude checks the ask against templates/custom-order-form.md: occasion, size or servings, the date they need it, and budget. Whatever the inquiry left out becomes friendly questions inside the reply.
3. If business/services.md lists a starting price for that kind of bake, the draft may mention it — plainly labeled as a starting point, with the final price yours once you've seen the full request. If services.md is silent on price, no number appears; Claude asks you first.
4. The draft never says the date works. It says you'll check the week and confirm — even when the calendar looks wide open, that call is yours.
5. If the customer mentions an allergy or a diet, the draft thanks them for flagging it and says you'll answer that personally. Claude never answers it, and never softens it into "should be fine".
6. Claude writes the reply in your voice: warm, short, sums up the order so far, asks the missing questions, and closes with you confirming the price and the pickup date.
7. Claude shows you the draft and stops. You edit or approve, then send it from your own account or phone.
8. Claude saves the running note to reports/ so the thread is easy to pick back up when the customer replies.
9. If something durable came up — a regular's usual, a new kind of bake you'd take again — Claude proposes the exact line for the right business/ file and waits for your yes.

## What you get

One file in reports/, named reports/custom-order-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md:

- The inquiry as it arrived.
- The order summary so far — occasion, size, date, budget — with the gaps marked plainly.
- The reply you approved, ready to copy and send.
- What's still waiting: on the customer, or on you (the price, the deposit, whether the date fits).

## Never

- Never accept, confirm, or turn down an order. The reply gathers and sums up; the yes to the customer is yours.
- Never promise a price, a pickup date or time, or deposit terms. A starting price from services.md is an estimate and says so; the final number is yours, fresh each time.
- Never say the week has room. Whether an order fits the ovens is your call, every time.
- Never answer an allergy or diet question, and never call a bake nut-free, gluten-free, or safe for anyone. The draft says you'll answer personally, and repeats only allergen words you wrote in business/brand.md, as your words.
- Never send the reply. Every message goes out from your hands.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
