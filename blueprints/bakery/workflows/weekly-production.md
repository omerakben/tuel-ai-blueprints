# Weekly production hour

One hour a week to plan the week's bakes, orders, and specials — Claude proposes, and you decide what the ovens do.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. Many bakers use the quiet stretch after the morning rush on their slowest day. Say "let's plan the week" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's plan in reports/, plus the week's daily closes: what sold out, what came home, which day surprised you. If there is no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Gather the week's commitments. Custom orders due, standing orders, preorders already in — from reports/, from assets/, and from you. Claude lists each with its day and name, exactly as written. If it can't find the week's orders, it asks; it never assumes a quiet week.
3. Propose the week. A day-by-day bake plan starting from the usual lineup in business/services.md, adjusted where the closes give a reason — every adjustment shown as a question with its reason, like "croissants came home three days running; bake fewer on weekdays?"
4. Pick a special together. Claude suggests one or two ideas from things that are real — a holiday coming, a custom bake that went well, something you mentioned wanting to try. You choose, or name your own. Nothing goes on the plan until you have picked.
5. Check the pantry. Claude runs the plan past business/suppliers.md and lists anything worth counting before the week starts — a "count these" list for skills/prepare-an-ingredient-order/, not an order. Claude never invents a count.
6. Approve the plan. You go day by day: change counts, move bakes, cut the special. Changed days come back for another look. Only a plan with your fresh yes gets saved.
7. Save it. The plan lands in reports/, and Claude offers one more thing: draft the weekend preorder post now, or leave it for the midweek push.

## What you get

- A day-by-day plan for the week saved as reports/weekly-production-[date].md: orders due with names, the counter lineup per day, the special if you picked one, and the "count these" list.
- A clear picture, before the week starts, of the one or two days that will be tight.

## Never

- Claude never confirms an order fits the week — flagging a tight day is as far as it goes. The ovens, the schedule, and the yes to any customer are yours.
- Claude never invents an order, an event, a count, or a reason. Every adjustment traces to a close, a file, or your words.
- A yes covers this week's plan only. Next week starts fresh, and staying quiet on a day is not a yes.
- Nothing in the plan reaches a customer or a supplier by itself. Posts, notes, and orders each get their own fresh yes.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
