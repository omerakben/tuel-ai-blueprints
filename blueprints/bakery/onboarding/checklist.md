# What to collect, in order

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Bakery name
- Kind of bakery (breads, cakes and pastries, a bit of everything) and the feel of the place in three words
- Days and opening hours
- The usual counter lineup, with what each bake sells for and roughly how many get made
- Whether custom orders are taken, and what the owner always needs to know before saying yes
- Who's in the kitchen (just the owner is fine)
- How custom orders reach the bakery today (messages, calls, email, the counter)

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full price list (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- House policies: how far ahead custom orders need to be placed, deposits, cancellations, pickup rules
- The regulars: names the owner wants remembered, their usual order, any standing weekend order
- Suppliers: who delivers flour, butter, dairy, boxes — and how orders get placed
- The exact words the owner uses about allergens (for example, "our kitchen handles nuts and wheat") — Claude repeats these words and never writes new ones
- Logo and a few photos the owner likes, dropped into `assets/`
- The bakery's look and voice: three words, colors, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, Yelp) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- Reorder points: how low flour, butter, or boxes get before it's time to order
- Wholesale or standing orders: any cafe or shop that takes a regular delivery
- Busiest and slowest days, as the owner sees them
- The bakery's story — how it started, what it's known for
- How each person takes their pay, only if the owner wants the daily close to track it
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (permits, insurance)
