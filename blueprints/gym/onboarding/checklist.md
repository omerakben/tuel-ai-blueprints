Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Gym name
- Kind of gym (weights, group classes, yoga, a mix) and the feel of the place in three words
- Days and opening hours, and roughly how the class timetable runs
- What's on offer — memberships, class packs, drop-ins — with price and what each includes
- Who coaches or teaches, what they're great at, which days (just you counts)
- How people join and book today (calls, messages, walk-ins, a booking tool)
- Which classes fill fast and which tend to have space

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full timetable and price list (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- The first-week pointers for new members: which class to start with, what to bring, where to park
- House policies: freezes and holds, cancellations, guest passes and trials, late arrivals, refunds
- The regulars: names the owner wants remembered, their usual class, how often they come
- How renewals work: how the owner keeps track of who's coming due, and how members usually pay
- Suppliers: who services the equipment, who delivers cleaning supplies and front-desk stock, and how orders get placed
- Logo and a few photos of the space the owner likes, dropped into `assets/`
- The gym's look and voice: three words, colors, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, elsewhere) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- Front-desk retail (drinks, bars, merch) and when to reorder
- Busiest and quietest times of day and week, as the owner sees them
- The gym's story — how it started, what it's known for
- How each coach takes their pay (per class, hourly), only if the owner wants the end-of-day close to track it
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (insurance, coach certifications, equipment servicing)
