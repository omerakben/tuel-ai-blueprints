# Welcome a new member

A new member just joined. Claude drafts the welcome note with your first-week pointers; you send it.

## When to run this

- Someone signed up today and you want them to feel expected, not processed.
- A trial member just decided to stay.
- A small batch of new-year joiners needs settling in, one note each.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — the first-week pointers you saved: which class to start with, what to bring, where to park.
- business/profile.md — your hours and how booking works.
- business/staff.md — so the note can name the coach they'll meet, if you want.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the welcome sounds like you.
- templates/welcome-note.md — the shape the note starts from.

Claude needs two things from you: the new member's first name, and what they signed up for. If the first-week pointers in services.md are still blank, Claude asks you for one or two rather than padding the note with generic advice.

## Steps

1. Tell Claude who joined and what they signed up for — a membership, a class pack, a trial.
2. Claude pulls the first-week pointers from services.md. Blank? Claude asks you for one or two real ones, and offers to save them for every future welcome — shown as exact lines first.
3. Claude drafts the note from templates/welcome-note.md: a warm hello, what happens in week one, one concrete pointer, and how to reach you with questions. Short — a welcome, not a handbook.
4. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Change it as many times as you like; nothing moves until you say it is good.
5. You send it yourself — text or email, from your own phone. Claude saves a copy to reports/.
6. If you want, Claude proposes a line for clients.md — their name and their usual, once they have one — and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

A ready-to-send note saved as reports/welcome-note-YYYY-MM-DD.md — who it is for, what they joined, and the approved note, ready to copy.

## Never

- Never invent what a membership includes or costs, or which class runs when. If it is not in business/ or something you just said, Claude asks.
- Never put a workout plan, an exercise recommendation, or diet advice in a welcome. What is right for their body is between them and their doctor or physio.
- Never promise results. The note welcomes them in — it does not sign them up for a transformation.
- Never mention fitness level, weight, or goals unless the owner asks for a specific line, and even then no promises attached.
- Never send anything. The note waits in reports/ until you send it yourself.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
