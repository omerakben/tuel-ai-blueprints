# Weekly bays hour

One hour a week to walk the board — Claude turns the loose ends into drafts, and you decide what goes out.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. Many owners use Monday before the doors open or the quiet stretch after lunch. Say "let's do the bays hour" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's bays-hour report in reports/ and checks what got sent, paid, or picked up. If there is no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Walk the board. You tell Claude what's in the shop: jobs open, jobs waiting on a customer's yes, jobs waiting on parts, cars done but not picked up, invoices still open. Claude sorts it into one list and asks about gaps — it never adds a job you didn't mention.
3. Nudge the waiting approvals. For each job waiting on a customer's yes, Claude drafts a short follow-up from templates/approval-request.md — a gentle second ask, never pressure, with the technician's findings quoted as they were said.
4. Update the parts waiters. Each car waiting on a part gets a status update drafted from templates/status-update.md. No arrival date from a supplier means the draft promises a follow-up, not a date.
5. Remind the unpaid. You name any invoices to nudge; Claude drafts each from templates/invoice-reminder.md. Only the ones you name.
6. Approve the batch. Everything lands in one place — every follow-up, update, and reminder. You go item by item: yes, no, or change it. Changed items come back for another look. An item you skip is a no.
7. Save the plan. Claude ends with a short plan — each approved item and when you mean to send it — saved to reports/ with the approved drafts underneath. You do the sending.

## What you get

- Follow-ups, status updates, and invoice reminders for the week, approved by you and ready to copy.
- A one-page plan saved as reports/weekly-bays-[date].md, with the approved drafts underneath it.

## Never

- Claude never sends or posts anything. Not one word leaves this folder without you.
- Claude never invents a job, a price, a part, or an arrival date. If it is not in your business/ files or something you just said, Claude asks.
- Claude never diagnoses and never calls a car safe, unsafe, or drivable in any draft — the technician's words, quoted, only.
- A yes covers that exact item, that week. Last week's yes approves nothing this week, and staying quiet on an item is a no.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
