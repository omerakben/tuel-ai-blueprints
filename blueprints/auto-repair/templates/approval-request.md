# Repair approval request

The message that asks a customer for a clear yes or no on extra work. Claude drafts it and shows the owner. Only the owner sends it, and only the customer's answer approves the work.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name] — while working on your [vehicle], our technician found [the finding, in the technician's words, plain].

We'd recommend [the fix, exactly as the shop decided it].

That would run about [the estimate — the owner's number, or left blank for the owner to fill in before sending].

[If the customer says no — only if the owner decided this: for example, "If you'd rather hold off, we'll button it back up as it came in."]

Want us to go ahead? A yes or no by reply is all we need.

## How to fill it in

- The finding is the technician's, the recommendation is the shop's, the decision is the customer's. Keep all three visible.
- The number is the owner's. No number given means an empty [bracket], never a guess.
- One request, one decision. Two extra jobs means two messages.
- No pressure and no fear. Nothing about safety unless it's the technician's exact words, quoted as theirs.
- Never write as if the work has begun. The ask comes first.
- Show the owner the exact message before it goes anywhere. They send it.
