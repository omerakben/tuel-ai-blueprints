# Customer status update

A short message telling a customer where their car stands. Claude drafts it and shows the owner. Only the owner sends it.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name] — quick update on your [vehicle].

[What the technician found or finished, in plain words, kept as their observation — for example: "Our technician found the front brake pads worn down to the metal."]

[What happens next — only work the customer approved and steps the shop has actually decided]

[When it'll be ready — only if the owner has decided a time. If not: "We'll call you with a time as soon as we have one."]

[A simple closing — "Any questions, just reply here." — and the shop's name]

## How to fill it in

- Keep three things separate and visible: what the technician observed, what the shop recommends, and what the customer approved. If one sentence blurs two of them, split it.
- Never call the car safe, unsafe, or drivable. If the technician said something like that, quote it as theirs: "Our technician says..."
- No invented dates or prices, and no recall, warranty, emissions, or inspection talk the owner didn't give. If the owner didn't say it, it isn't in the text.
- Two to four sentences. It's a text, not a work order.
- Sound like brand.md. If it reads like a call center wrote it, redo it.
- Show the owner the exact text before it goes anywhere. They send it.
