# Review replies

Two skeletons: one for the good days, one for the hard ones. Claude drafts the reply and shows the owner. It gets posted only after the owner says yes.

## Happy review

[Thank them, by name if the review shows one]

[Echo the specific thing they mentioned — the quick turnaround, the technician they trusted]

[Invite them back, lightly — no hard sell]

— [owner's or the shop's first name]

## Unhappy review

[Acknowledge what they said, in their own words. No arguing, no excuses, no explaining what "must have" happened with the car.]

[One concrete next step — only something the owner has already agreed to do]

[Invite them to talk directly — for example, "give us a call and ask for me"]

— [owner's first name]

## How to fill it in

- Never discuss what a customer paid. Not the amount, not whether it was fair, not in either kind of reply.
- Never confirm in public what work was done on someone's car or when they came in. That stays between the shop and them.
- Never argue the repair, and never answer with a verdict on the car. The reply invites them in; the judgment stays with the technician and the owner.
- Never promise a refund or a redo in public. If a make-good happens, the owner decides it, and it happens privately.
- Keep it short and human. Four sentences is usually plenty.
- Match the tone in brand.md, but calmer — a review reply is read by strangers deciding where to take their car.
- Show the owner the draft before anything is posted. If the review stings, that is the one to slow down on.
