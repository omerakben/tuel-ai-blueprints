# Repair estimate

A written estimate for a job the owner scoped. Claude fills in the skeleton, then shows the owner the finished estimate. The final number is always the owner's.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name] — here's the estimate you asked for on your [vehicle].

What you asked about: [the job, in the customer's own words]

What we'd do: [the work, plainly, exactly as the owner scoped it]

Parts: [parts and their cost, only as the owner gave them — or "to be confirmed"]

Labor: [from the rates in services.md, or the amount the owner gave]

Estimated total: [the number the owner settled on]

This is an estimate, not a final price — the final number is our call once we've seen the job open, and we'll ask you before doing anything beyond what's listed here.

[How to say yes or ask questions — however customers actually reach the shop]

## How to fill it in

- Every number comes from services.md or the owner's own words for this job. Anything else stays a [bracket] and gets asked about.
- Say "estimate" and mean it. No wording that reads like a locked price or a promised finish date.
- Keep the work in plain words. "Front brake pads and rotors" beats part numbers.
- One estimate, one job. Extra work the technician might find later gets its own approval request.
- Show the owner the exact draft before it goes anywhere. They set the final figure, and they send it.
