# Invoice reminders

Friendly nudges for unpaid invoices — only the ones you name. Claude drafts; you send.

## When to run this

- An invoice has sat unpaid longer than feels right.
- The weekly bays hour turned up open invoices you want chased kindly.
- You'd rather send a warm nudge today than make an awkward call next month.

## What Claude reads first

- business/policies.md — how customers pay, and anything you've set about reminders.
- business/clients.md — the customer, so the tone fits how well you know them.
- business/brand.md — how the shop talks.
- templates/invoice-reminder.md — the shape each reminder starts from.

Then from you: which invoices. Claude never combs records to decide who owes — you name them, one by one, with the amount and the work.

## Steps

1. Name the invoice: who, roughly when, what the work was, and the amount still open. Claude works from what you say — it never guesses an amount or a date.
2. One customer at a time, Claude drafts a short reminder that sounds like you: warm, no guilt, with the amount and the work named plainly and the ways to pay exactly as you offer them.
3. If this isn't the first reminder, tell Claude — the second nudge can be a shade firmer, still kind. It never threatens.
4. Claude shows you each draft and waits. Cross out any you've changed your mind about.
5. You send the ones you approve, from your own phone or email. Claude never sends.
6. Claude saves the batch to reports/ so you can tick them off as payments land.
7. If a customer pays or pushes back, tell Claude — it notes the outcome for next time and proposes any durable note for business/clients.md, written only on your yes.

## What you get

One file per run, named reports/invoice-reminder-YYYY-MM-DD.md: each reminder as you approved it, who it's for, and space to mark paid, promised, or no answer.

## Never

- Never chase an invoice you didn't name. No sweeps, no lists Claude built on its own.
- Never invent or recalculate an amount, and never add a late fee or interest unless business/policies.md sets one and you said yes for this exact reminder.
- Never threaten — no collections talk, no legal talk, no "final notice" theater. If an invoice needs more than a friendly nudge, that's a conversation with you, maybe with a professional.
- Never mention what the work found or fixed beyond what's needed to name the invoice — the customer's business stays theirs.
- Never send a reminder, and never set one up to go out later.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
