# Reply to a review

You paste in a review from Google or Yelp, and Claude drafts a steady reply in your shop's own voice for you to post.

## When to run this

- A new review just landed, glowing or rough.
- A rough one came in and you'd rather see a calm draft than type one while heated.
- An older review has sat unanswered and it bothers you.
- You want your replies to sound like one voice, week after week.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how your shop talks, so the reply sounds like you and not like a form letter.
- business/staff.md — to name the right technician when the review mentions one.
- business/services.md — to get the names of jobs right.
- business/policies.md — your hard limits, in case the review touches warranty or payment.
- templates/review-reply.md — the shape each reply starts from.

You paste or say: the full review text, which site it's from, the star rating if there is one, and anything you remember about that visit. Claude never needs your Google or Yelp password — it works only with the text you paste.

## Steps

1. Paste the review and tell Claude which site it came from.
2. Claude reads it and takes its temperature. If anything is unclear — who worked on the car, what the job was — Claude asks you before writing a word.
3. For a happy review, Claude drafts a short thank-you that echoes the specific thing they praised, using only names that match your business files, and invites them back.
4. For an unhappy or mixed review, Claude drafts a calm reply that thanks them for telling you and moves the conversation off the page: the draft invites them to reach the shop directly so you can talk it through. It doesn't argue, doesn't make excuses, and never explains what "must have" gone wrong with the car.
5. The draft never offers a refund, a redo, or a discount unless you decided that for this exact review. If you do, tell Claude your exact offer and it goes in word for word.
6. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Ask for a shorter one, a warmer one — nothing moves until you say it's good.
7. You post the reply on the site yourself. Claude never posts.
8. Claude saves a copy to reports/ so the next reply can match the tone you liked.

## What you get

One file per review, named reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the original review, which site it came from, the reply you approved, and whether you posted it.

## Never

- Never draft a fake review, and never write anything meant to look like it came from a customer.
- Never confirm in public what work was done on someone's car, when they came in, or what they paid — that stays between you and them.
- Never diagnose in a reply, and never say anything about whether their car is or was safe to drive. If the review argues the repair, the reply invites them in — the judgment stays with the technician and you.
- Never offer a refund, redo, or discount in a reply unless you decided that for this exact review.
- Never suggest paying or rewarding anyone for a review, and never offer anything in exchange for changing or removing one.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
