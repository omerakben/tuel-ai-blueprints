# Inquiry reply

Turns a fresh event inquiry into a warm reply that gathers what you actually need — the date, the headcount, the venue, the budget — without reading like a form.

## When to run this

Someone just asked about an event. That might be an email you paste in, an Instagram message, a note from your website form, or your own retelling of a phone call. Run it while the inquiry is warm — same day beats next week.

A follow-up counts too: if a lead answered some questions and left others open, run the same steps on what's still missing.

## What Claude reads first

- business/profile.md — what you plan and the next step you like to offer new clients.
- business/services.md — your packages, so the reply points at the right fit without quoting a number you didn't give.
- business/brand.md — how you sound, so the reply reads like you wrote it.
- business/policies.md — anything the reply should respect, like when you take a deposit.
- templates/inquiry-reply.md — the shape the reply starts from.

## Steps

1. Paste the inquiry, or tell Claude about the call in your own words.
2. Claude repeats back what it heard: who's asking, what kind of event, and which of the four essentials they already answered — date, headcount, venue status, budget range. If the note is unclear, Claude asks you, not the lead.
3. Claude picks the one question that matters most right now — usually the date. The rest waits for the next note. Warm beats thorough; nobody answers a quiz.
4. If the reply needs a fact you haven't given — a price, whether one of your dates is open, anything about availability — Claude stops and asks you first. It never guesses in a lead's direction.
5. Claude drafts the reply from templates/inquiry-reply.md: one warm line about their event, a true line about how you work, the one clear ask, and the next step from profile.md. It shows you the draft and waits. You send it from your own email or account, or say what to change and Claude redrafts.
6. Claude saves the inquiry and the approved draft to reports/, so when the lead replies in three days you have the thread.
7. If the inquiry brought something durable — a lead and event worth tracking — Claude offers the exact line for business/clients.md and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

- The reply, ready for you to copy and send yourself.
- One file in reports/, named like reports/inquiry-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the inquiry, what's known and missing of the four essentials, and the approved draft.

## Never

- Never send the reply — every message leaves from your hands, every time.
- Never quote a price, package, or policy that isn't in business/ or in what you just said.
- Never tell a lead a date is open or held. Your calendar is yours; Claude only knows what you've confirmed.
- Never pack every question into one message. One clear ask leads; the rest can wait.
- Never repeat a lead's budget to anyone else, and never push past a "we're just looking".

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
