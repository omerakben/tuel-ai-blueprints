# Planning timeline

Lays out a booked event backward from the big day — what happens when, with your confirmed dates locked in and [open slots] for the rest.

## When to run this

An event just went from "proposal out" to "booked" — that's the best moment. Also good when a booked event feels foggy and you want the road ahead on one page, or when a date moved and the whole plan needs to shift with it.

## What Claude reads first

- business/clients.md — the event, its date, and what's been decided so far.
- business/suppliers.md — your vendor book, so the timeline names who does what.
- business/staff.md — who's on the crew, so day-of steps land with the right person.
- business/policies.md — your deposit and payment rhythm, so money milestones land on the page too.
- Your notes on this event — paste anything you have. Claude works only from what you've confirmed.

## Steps

1. Confirm the anchor. Claude asks for the event date if it isn't saved, and checks it back with you. No confirmed date, no timeline — this playbook stops rather than building on a maybe.
2. Gather what's fixed. Claude lists what you've confirmed so far — the venue, vendors you've booked, tastings, fittings, whatever this event has — and asks about the obvious gaps, one at a time.
3. Claude drafts the timeline working backward from the big day: the milestones, who each one involves, and when it needs to happen. Dates you confirmed appear as dates. Everything else appears as an [open slot] with a plain note, like [book the tasting — roughly two months out].
4. Money milestones come from business/policies.md and your notes: when deposits and balances come due, shown as reminders for you — never as anything Claude collects.
5. Claude shows you the whole draft and waits. You move things, cut things, and add the pieces only you know. Claude updates it until it matches how you actually run an event.
6. The approved timeline is saved to reports/. When a date firms up later, tell Claude — it updates the timeline and shows you the changed lines before saving.

## What you get

- One page in reports/, named like reports/planning-timeline-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the event, the big day, and the working-backward plan — confirmed dates locked in, [open slots] plainly marked, and who each step involves.
- A short list of the gaps only you can fill, one per line, so the next chat starts where this one stopped.

## Never

- Never fill an [open slot] with a guess. A hole in the plan beats a made-up date.
- Never mark a vendor or venue as booked, held, or available unless you said you confirmed it.
- Never book, sign, or commit anything on this timeline — every hold, every contract, and every deposit is yours to make.
- Never turn a money milestone into an ask. Reminders are for you; collecting stays in your hands.
- Never send the timeline to a client or vendor itself — you share it when and how you choose.
- Never carry what the client plans to spend onto a page a vendor will see. That stays between you and the client unless you say otherwise.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
