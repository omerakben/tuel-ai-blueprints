# Event proposal

Builds the proposal skeleton from your notes — the concept, what you handle, and budget lines left open for your numbers.

## When to run this

You've talked with the lead — a call, a coffee, an email thread — and it's time to put a proposal in front of them. Run it while your notes are fresh; rough notes are fine.

## What Claude reads first

- business/profile.md and business/brand.md — what you plan and how you sound.
- business/services.md — your packages, so the proposal matches what you actually sell.
- business/clients.md — anything already saved about this lead and their event.
- business/policies.md — deposits and payment rhythm, so the proposal says what happens after a yes.
- templates/event-proposal.md — the skeleton the proposal starts from.

## Steps

1. Tell Claude about the event in your own words: what they want, what you'd handle, what you talked about. Paste your notes if you have them — messy is fine.
2. Claude repeats back the concept as it heard it: the event, the feel, the pieces that matter to this client. Correct anything before it goes further.
3. Claude drafts the proposal from templates/event-proposal.md: the concept in words the client will recognize, what you handle and what stays with them, and the budget as labeled lines with [open slots] where the numbers go. Claude never fills a number in — those are yours.
4. If a piece is missing — which package this maps to, whether the date is confirmed, what the client said about budget — Claude stops and asks you rather than papering over it.
5. Claude shows you the full draft and waits. You fill in the numbers and adjust the words; Claude tidies the final version until you're happy.
6. The finished proposal is saved to reports/. You send it yourself, from your own email, when you're ready.
7. If the conversation settled something durable — the event date, a decision, what matters most to this client — Claude offers the exact lines for business/clients.md and writes them only after your yes.

## What you get

- A proposal with the words done and every number left as an [open slot] for you.
- One file in reports/, named like reports/event-proposal-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the concept, the draft, and a short list of anything still unknown.

## Never

- Never fill in a price, fee, or budget number. Every figure comes from your hands, and the proposal says plainly these are estimates — the final figure is your call once the plan is settled.
- Never promise a vendor, a venue, or a date you haven't confirmed. "We'll ask them" is honest; "they're yours" is not.
- Never send the proposal — you send it, from your own account, when it's ready.
- Never sign or commit anything, and never write words that read like a signed commitment.
- Never put what this client plans to spend in front of anyone else unless you say so.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
