# Vendor brief

One clear page per vendor — what you need, when you need it, and how to reply — ready for you to send to your florist, caterer, or venue.

## When to run this

A booked event needs vendors moving: you're about to ask the florist for arrangements, the caterer for a menu, the venue for load-in details. Run it once for a single vendor, or in a batch when an event kicks off.

## What Claude reads first

- business/clients.md — the event: its date, venue status, what's been decided.
- business/suppliers.md — this vendor: what they do, how you work together, your notes.
- business/brand.md — how you sound, so the brief reads like it came from you.
- templates/vendor-brief.md — the shape every brief starts from.
- Your notes for this ask — what exactly you want from this vendor this time.

## Steps

1. Name the event and the vendor. Claude pulls what it has from business/ and asks for whatever the brief still needs — quantities, styles, the moment on the day this vendor owns.
2. If a material fact is missing — the event date isn't confirmed, the venue isn't settled, you haven't said what you want — Claude stops and asks. A brief built on maybes wastes the vendor's time and yours.
3. Claude drafts one page from templates/vendor-brief.md: the event and date, what you need from this vendor, when you need it by, where things happen, and how you'd like them to reply. Contact notes come from your vendor book.
4. Claude keeps the client's budget off the brief. If you want a figure included for this vendor, say so — Claude adds exactly the figure you gave, nothing more.
5. Claude shows you the draft and waits. You adjust it, then send it yourself — by email, by text, however you and that vendor talk.
6. Repeat for the next vendor if it's a batch. Each brief is its own draft with its own yes — approving the florist's page approves nothing else.
7. All briefs from the run are saved to reports/ so you can track who has replied.

## What you get

- One ready-to-send page per vendor, saved together in reports/, named like reports/vendor-brief-YYYY-MM-DD.md: each brief, plus a short checklist of who it's for and what you asked them.
- Questions for you wherever a fact was missing, one per line, so nothing gets papered over.

## Never

- Never send a brief — you send it, to the vendor you choose, when you choose.
- Never book, hold, or confirm a vendor. A brief asks; it never commits. The words "you're booked" don't belong in it.
- Never state that a vendor is available or assume they'll say yes — the brief asks for their availability, it doesn't announce it.
- Never include what the client plans to spend unless you said to, and then only the exact figure you gave for this vendor.
- Never invent quantities, styles, or dates. If it isn't confirmed in business/ or by you, it's an [open slot] or a question for you.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
