# What to collect, in order

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Business name
- Kinds of events (weddings, parties, corporate, a mix) and the feel of the events in three words
- Packages and how the owner charges — each package, what it covers, a rough price
- Who's on the crew — just the owner? that's fine
- Events booked right now: client, kind of event, date, and where each stands
- How new inquiries arrive (email, website form, Instagram messages)
- The next step the owner offers someone new (a call, a coffee, a video chat)

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full package sheet or price list (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- House policies: deposits, payment rhythm, cancellations, late changes
- The vendor book: the go-to florist, caterer, photographer, and venues — what each is great at and how the owner reaches them
- For each booked event: which dates and vendors the owner has confirmed so far
- Logo and a few photos from past events, dropped into `assets/`
- How the business sounds: three words, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, The Knot, WeddingWire) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- The deposit rhythm: when the owner invoices, and how far ahead they like reminders drafted
- Busy and slow seasons, as the owner sees them
- The business's story — how it started, what it's known for
- Preferred venues' quirks worth remembering (load-in times, house rules)
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (insurance, permits)
