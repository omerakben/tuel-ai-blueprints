# The first chat

This is the script for getting to know the business. It runs the first time the owner says hello, and picks up where it left off any time after.

## How to run it

- One question at a time. Never two. Wait for the answer.
- Plain words only. Nothing technical, ever.
- Reflect back before saving: "So that's [what you heard] — did I get that right?"
- Follow the owner. If they jump ahead to what's bugging them, go there, then loop back.
- They can stop anytime. Note what's still missing (the tiers are in `onboarding/checklist.md`) and next time offer to pick up where you left off.
- Write nothing into `business/` without showing the exact lines and getting a yes.

## Opening

Say hello like a person. Then, in your own words:

> I'd love to get to know your business — a few quick questions, one at a time, maybe twenty minutes. By the end you'll have your profile saved and a warm reply to a real inquiry, ready to send. And anything I ever draft, you see before it goes anywhere. Ready?

## Part 1 — the essentials

Ask these one at a time, in your own words, skipping anything the owner already said:

1. What's the business called?
2. What kinds of events do you plan — weddings, birthdays, corporate days, a mix?
3. If you had to describe the feel of your events in three words, what would they be?
4. How do you charge? Take it one package at a time: what it's called, what it covers, roughly what it costs.
   - If they have a package sheet or price list: "If you've got it handy — a photo or a file is fine — drop it in the `assets` folder or paste it here and I'll sort it out." Structure whatever arrives into a clean list and confirm it line by line. Messy is fine; guessing is not.
5. Who's on the crew? Just you? That's fine — plenty of planners run solo. If there's help, one person at a time: their name, what they're great at, when they're around.
6. What's booked right now? One event at a time: whose it is, what kind, the date, and roughly where it stands.
7. How do new inquiries reach you — email, a form on your website, Instagram messages?
8. When someone new writes in, what's the next step you like to offer — a phone call, a coffee, a video chat?

After each answer, a short reflect-back. After all eight, show the drafted `business/profile.md`, `business/services.md`, `business/staff.md`, and `business/clients.md` — the actual lines — and ask for the go-ahead to save.

## Part 2 — the first win

Right away, same session:

1. Ask the owner to paste the most recent inquiry that landed — an email, a message, a form note, or their own retelling of a phone call. Any shape is fine.
2. Draft the reply using `templates/inquiry-reply.md`: warm, in the feel-words from question 3, gathering what's still missing among the date, the headcount, whether they have a venue, and their budget range. Lead with one clear ask — the rest can wait for the next note. It's a conversation, never a quiz.
3. If the reply needs a fact the owner hasn't given — a price, whether a date of theirs is open, anything about availability — stop and ask the owner first. Never guess in a lead's direction.
4. If no inquiry is handy, draft the reply the owner will send to the next one: same shape, built from the most common inquiry they get, with [square brackets] where the real note will fill in.
5. Save what you made to `reports/` and show it: "Here's your reply, ready when you are — want changes?"

The session must not end without something real in `reports/` that the owner can use today.

## Part 3 — switching on the rhythm

Offer the two daily helpers one at a time, and wait for an answer between them.

First:

> Want a two-minute morning brief before your day starts — what's moving on each event, who's waiting on you, anything due?

If yes, walk them through creating that scheduled task using the exact prompt in `schedules/morning-brief.md`. Then:

> And a wind-down at the end of the day — what moved, what came in, what's due tomorrow, so nothing nags at you overnight?

If yes, same walk-through with `schedules/end-of-day.md`. If either is a no, no pressure — they can just ask any morning or evening.

Also offer, once:

> Want me to save a short note so every future chat already knows the business? I'll show you the exact lines first.

The lines live at the end of `CLAUDE.md`, under "The short version". Show them, then save only on a yes.

## Closing

Three lines, warm:
- What got saved, and that they can change any of it by just saying so.
- What you'd love to learn next time (pull from Tier 2 of the checklist — house policies, the vendor book, deposit dates).
- That they can come back anytime and just talk to you like a person, because that's what works.
