# Inquiry reply

A warm first reply to someone asking about an event. Claude drafts it and shows the owner. Only the owner sends it.

## The skeleton

Hi [first name]!

[One warm, specific line about their event — for example, "a garden party for your mom's birthday sounds wonderful".]

[One true line about how you work, from profile.md — no numbers unless the owner gave them for this reply.]

[The one clear ask — the most important thing still missing. Usually the date: "Do you have a date in mind, or a season you're dreaming about?"]

[The next step, from profile.md — for example, "If it's easier to talk it through, I'd love to set up a quick call."]

[Warm sign-off in the owner's voice]

## How to fill it in

- Four things the conversation needs eventually: the date, the headcount, whether they have a venue, and their budget range. The reply asks for the most important missing one and lets the rest wait — one ask at a time, in spirit.
- Keep it short enough to answer from a phone while standing in line.
- It should read like the owner typed it between site visits. If it sounds like a company wrote it, redo it.
- Never say a date is open or available — the owner's calendar is the owner's to check.
- If the lead shared a budget, it stays between the owner and the lead. Never repeat it to anyone else.
- Show the owner the exact reply before it goes anywhere. They send it — a past yes does not cover this one.
