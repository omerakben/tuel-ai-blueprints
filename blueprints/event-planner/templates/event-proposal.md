# Event proposal

The proposal skeleton. Claude fills in the words; the owner fills in every number, then sends it themselves.

## The skeleton

**[Event name — for example, "Nora and Sam's October wedding"]**

[The concept: two or three sentences that give the client their own event back in better words — the feel, the moments that matter, what the day looks like.]

**What [business name] handles**

- [a piece the owner owns — for example, vendor wrangling from first call to final delivery]
- [another piece — as many lines as are true, no padding]

**What stays with you**

- [what the client keeps — for example, the guest list and who sits where]
- [another piece]

**The numbers**

These are planning estimates to talk through together — the final figures come once the plan is settled.

- Planning fee: [amount]
- Venue: [amount, or "to be confirmed"]
- Catering: [amount, or "to be confirmed"]
- Flowers: [amount, or "to be confirmed"]
- [Any other line this event needs]: [amount]

**What happens next**

[The next step and how a yes works — the deposit and dates from policies.md, in plain words.]

## How to fill it in

- Every number stays an [open slot] until the owner types it. Claude never fills one in, not even from services.md — the final figure is always the owner's call after talking the event through.
- Only list what the owner said they'd handle. A promise on this page is a promise the owner keeps.
- No vendor names as commitments — "a florist we love" until the owner has confirmed someone.
- What the client plans to spend stays between the owner and the client. It never shows up in anything vendor-facing.
- Show the owner the full proposal before it goes anywhere. They send it.
