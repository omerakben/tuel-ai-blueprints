# Vendor brief

One page that tells a vendor exactly what's needed and when. Claude drafts it; the owner sends it. It asks — it never books.

## The skeleton

**Event:** [what and when — only dates the owner confirmed]

**Where:** [the venue, once the owner says it can be shared]

**What we need from you:** [the ask, plainly — arrangements, a menu for a headcount, load-in support]

**When we need it:** [the dates and times the owner confirmed]

**Good to know:** [contact notes and quirks from the owner's vendor book]

**Could you let us know:** [what the vendor should reply with — availability, options, a quote — and by when]

## How to fill it in

- One vendor, one page. The florist's brief and the caterer's brief never share details they don't need.
- The brief asks whether the vendor is available. It never announces that they are, and it never says "you're booked" — booking is the owner's move, made outside this page.
- The client's budget stays off the page unless the owner says to include a figure, and then only the exact figure they gave.
- Every date on the page is one the owner confirmed. No "probably" dates.
- Show the owner the finished brief before it goes anywhere. They send it.
