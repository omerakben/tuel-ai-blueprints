# Weekly merchandising hour

One hour a week to walk the shop on paper — what moved, what sat, what goes front and center, and the week's posts. Claude drafts; you decide everything.

## When

Once a week, at a time you pick. Many owners use the quiet morning before opening or the slow stretch after lunch. Say "let's do the merchandising hour" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's merchandising plan in reports/ and the week's daily closes, and checks what went out and what got attention. If there is no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. What moved, what sat. You tell Claude — from your till report or your own read of the racks. Claude sorts it into two plain lists and never guesses which is which.
3. Pick the story of the week. Claude suggests one or two front-and-center ideas from real stock — a line that just landed, a restock, a season turning — and says why. You choose, or name your own. Nothing moves forward until you have picked.
4. Plan the floor. Claude sketches a short plan for the window and the front table around the story you picked. Every line is a suggestion — you know the shop, and you move the pieces.
5. Face the slow movers. For pieces that sat, Claude lists options as questions: move it up front, restyle it, pair it with something new, or mark it down. The markdown call and the new price are yours alone — Claude never sets or suggests a number.
6. Draft the week's posts. Claude writes 2 or 3 captions using templates/arrival-post.md, in your voice from business/brand.md. Prices and sizes come from your files or from you, never from memory. A photo from assets/ can go with a caption if you point one out.
7. Approve the batch. Claude lays everything out in one place — the floor plan and every caption. You go item by item: yes, no, or change it. Changed items come back for another look. An item you skip is a no. Only items with your fresh yes make the plan.
8. Save. Claude ends with a one-page plan: the story of the week, the floor moves, each approved caption and the day you'll post it. You do the posting. It all goes to reports/.

## What you get

- A one-page floor-and-posts plan saved as reports/weekly-merchandising-[date].md, with the approved captions underneath it.
- 2 or 3 captions ready to copy, plus your slow-mover decisions written down so next week starts smarter.

## Never

- Claude never posts or sends anything. Not one word leaves this folder without you.
- Claude never sets a price, a markdown, or a sale. It can ask "is it time to mark this down?" — the answer and the number are yours.
- Claude never invents what sold, what sat, or what's in stock. Your till, your racks, your words.
- A yes covers that exact item, that week. Last week's yes approves nothing this week, and staying quiet on an item is a no.
- No claims about a piece's fabric, maker, or origin in any caption unless they come from the tag, the vendor sheet, or you.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
