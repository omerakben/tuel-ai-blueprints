# Draft a review reply

You paste in a review from Google or Yelp, and Claude drafts a reply in your shop's own voice for you to post.

## When to run this

- A new review just landed on Google or Yelp.
- A rough review came in and you would rather see a calm draft than type one while upset.
- An older review has sat unanswered and it bothers you.
- You want your replies to sound like one voice, not five different moods.

## What Claude reads first

- business/brand.md — how your shop talks, so the reply sounds like you and not like a form letter.
- business/services.md — your lines and categories, so names come out right.
- business/policies.md — your return and exchange rules, in case the review touches on them.
- templates/review-reply.md — the shape each reply starts from.

You paste or say: the full review text, which site it is from, the star rating if there is one, and anything you remember about that visit. If a detail is missing, Claude asks you rather than guessing.

Claude never needs your Google or Yelp password. It works only with the text you paste.

## Steps

1. Paste the review and tell Claude which site it came from.
2. Claude reads it and takes its temperature. If anything is unclear — which piece they mean, whether a return was involved — Claude asks you before writing a word.
3. For a happy review, Claude drafts a short thank-you that echoes the piece or the visit the reviewer mentioned, using only details that match your files or your words, and invites them back in.
4. For an unhappy or mixed review, Claude drafts a calm reply that thanks them for telling you and moves the conversation off the page: the draft asks them to reach the shop at [your preferred contact] so you can sort it out directly. It does not argue and it does not make excuses. It never offers a refund, a return exception, or a replacement, unless you decide that yourself. If you do, tell Claude your exact offer and it goes into the draft word for word.
5. Claude shows you the draft and waits. Ask for changes as many times as you like — a shorter version, a warmer one. Nothing moves until you say the draft is good.
6. You post the reply on Google or Yelp yourself. Claude never posts it for you.
7. Claude saves a copy to reports/ so you have a record of what was said.
8. If the review points to something worth remembering, like a third complaint about the same line, Claude proposes a note for your business files and writes it only if you agree.

## What you get

A ready-to-post reply, saved as reports/review-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md, one file per review. The file holds the original review, which site it came from, the reply you approved, and whether you posted it — so the next time a similar review lands, Claude can match the tone you liked.

## Never

- Never draft a fake review, and never write anything meant to look like it came from a customer.
- Never offer a refund, a return exception, a discount, or a replacement in a reply unless you decided that for this exact review.
- Never suggest paying or rewarding anyone for a review, and never offer anything in exchange for changing or removing one.
- Never confirm in public what a customer bought, what they paid, or when they came in — that stays between you and them.
- Never defend a piece with claims about its fabric, maker, or quality that did not come from the tag, the vendor sheet, or the owner.
- Never restate your return terms loosely — if the reply mentions them, the words come from business/policies.md or from you.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
