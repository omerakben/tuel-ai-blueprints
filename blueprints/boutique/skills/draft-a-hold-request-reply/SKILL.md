# Draft a hold-request reply

A customer asks "can you hold it for me?" — Claude drafts the reply from your hold rules, and the hold only becomes real when you set the piece aside yourself.

## When to run this

- A hold request lands in your messages or email and you want the reply worded right.
- You're mid-rush and would rather approve a reply than compose one.
- The request is tricky — a piece you're not sure is still on the rack, or someone asking to hold longer than you usually do.

## What Claude reads first

- business/policies.md — your hold rules: how long you hold, and whether anything is needed to hold it.
- business/brand.md — how the shop talks, so the reply sounds like you.
- business/clients.md — in case the request comes from a regular you've told Claude about.
- templates/hold-reply.md — the shape every reply starts from.

From you: the pasted message, and whether the piece is actually still there — Claude cannot see your racks.

## Steps

1. Paste the message, or describe the call in your own words.
2. Claude repeats back what it heard: which piece, which size, how long they want it held. If a piece is missing, Claude asks you rather than guessing.
3. Claude checks business/policies.md for your hold rules. If there's no hold policy written down, Claude asks what you want to offer this time — it never invents a window or a deposit.
4. Claude asks whether the piece is still on the rack in that size. Until you've checked, the draft says you're checking — it never tells the customer the piece is theirs.
5. Claude drafts the reply using templates/hold-reply.md: warm, short, your hold terms stated plainly, nothing promised that you haven't confirmed.
6. Claude shows you the draft and waits. You send it from your own phone or account — Claude never sends a message.
7. The hold becomes real only when you say you've set the piece aside. Until then, Claude calls it "requested", never "held".
8. Claude saves the exchange to reports/. If the customer is a regular worth remembering, Claude offers one line for business/clients.md and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

One file saved as reports/hold-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md: the request as it came in, the reply you approved, and whether you confirmed the hold — so if the customer comes back three days later, the thread is right there.

## Never

- Never tell a customer a piece is held, reserved, or waiting — the hold is real only after the owner sets the piece aside and says so.
- Never promise a piece is available, in any size, until the owner has checked the rack.
- Never invent a hold window, a deposit, or an exception — hold terms come from business/policies.md or the owner's fresh call.
- Never stretch return or exchange terms in a reply to smooth things over — those come from the owner, not from Claude.
- Never send the reply — the owner sends every message, every time.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
