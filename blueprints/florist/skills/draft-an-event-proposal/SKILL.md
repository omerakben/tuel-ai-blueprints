# Draft an event proposal

Turns a wedding or event inquiry into a proposal skeleton — the looks in words, every quantity and price as a [slot] for you to fill and confirm.

## When to run this

A wedding or event inquiry lands: an email, a message, notes from a consult, or a couple who just left the shop. Run it while the conversation is fresh, so the details they gave don't slip away.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — what the shop offers for events, and any ranges you've written down.
- business/policies.md — deposits for events, lead times, and your substitution wording.
- business/brand.md — your voice, so the proposal reads like you wrote it.
- templates/event-proposal.md — the skeleton every proposal starts from.
- assets/ — photos of past work, if you point at ones to include.

## Steps

1. Paste the inquiry or your consult notes, or just talk it through in your own words.
2. Claude repeats back what it heard: the date they asked about, the venue they named, the feel they described, and the pieces they mentioned — bouquets, ceremony, tables, anything else. Whatever's missing becomes a question list for your next conversation with them, never a guess.
3. Claude drafts the proposal from templates/event-proposal.md, in your voice. The looks are described in plain words from what the couple or host said. Every quantity sits in a [slot]. Every price sits in a [slot]. The date and venue read as "as discussed — to be confirmed", because confirming them is yours.
4. Claude folds in what policies.md says about deposits and substitutions, in your own written words. If those lines are still blank, they become questions for you, not filler.
5. Claude shows you the skeleton and stops. You fill the numbers — quantities and prices are yours — and say what to change.
6. Once you've filled it in and given the go-ahead on the words, you send it yourself. The proposal says plainly it's an estimate, and that the final price is your call once the details are set.
7. If the event books, Claude offers one line for business/clients.md and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

- The proposal skeleton, then the finished version once you've filled the [slots], saved as reports/event-proposal-YYYY-MM-DD.md.
- The open-questions list for the couple or host, ready for your next conversation.

## Never

- Never confirm the event date, and never confirm venue access or timing — you sort that with the venue.
- Never fill in a quantity or a price. Those [slots] are yours alone.
- Never promise a specific stem for a specific date. Flowers are seasonal; what you promise is your call, in your words.
- Never present the proposal as a quote. It's an estimate until you say otherwise, and it says so.
- Never send the proposal — it leaves the shop from your hands.
- Never invent a deposit rule or a substitution promise. If policies.md is blank there, Claude asks you.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
