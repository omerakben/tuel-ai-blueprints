# Draft a custom order reply

Turns an order inquiry into a warm reply that gathers what you need — occasion, date, budget, and the feel — without promising anything you haven't checked.

## When to run this

A customer is asking about an order. That might be a text or a direct message you paste in, an email, a voicemail you sum up in your own words, or someone who stopped by the counter while your hands were full of stems.

It's also the first win in the first chat: paste in a real inquiry and walk out with the reply drafted.

## What Claude reads first

- business/services.md — what the shop offers and the usual ranges, so the reply talks about real things.
- business/policies.md — lead times, delivery rules, and your substitution wording, if you've written them down.
- business/brand.md — how you talk, so the reply sounds like you.
- business/clients.md — in case it's a regular with a usual order.
- templates/order-form.md — the details a finished order needs, so the reply asks for the right ones.

## Steps

1. Paste the inquiry, or describe the call or walk-in in your own words.
2. Claude repeats back what it heard: who's asking, what for, and when — then lists what's still missing among the four things every order needs: occasion, date, budget, and the feel they're after. Missing pieces become friendly questions in the reply, never guesses.
3. Claude checks services.md for what fits. If the request is something the shop doesn't offer or hasn't listed, Claude asks you before writing a word about it.
4. Claude drafts the reply in your voice: a warm thanks, one or two real options from your list if they fit, the questions for whatever's missing, and how ordering works from policies.md. Any date reads as "let me check and confirm" — never as booked. No stem is promised. If a price range from services.md helps, it appears as "usually around [range]" with the final price left as your call.
5. Claude shows you the draft and stops. You send it from your own phone or inbox, or say what to change and Claude redrafts.
6. As details firm up, Claude fills in templates/order-form.md with what the customer actually said and shows you the order sheet to keep or correct.
7. If the customer is a regular worth remembering, Claude offers one line for business/clients.md — the exact words — and writes it only after your yes.

## What you get

- The reply, ready to copy and send yourself.
- A note saved as reports/order-reply-YYYY-MM-DD.md holding the inquiry, the reply you approved, and the order form as far as it's filled in. Handy when the customer answers two days later and you need the thread back.

## Never

- Never confirm a delivery date or time — you do that, after you've checked your week.
- Never promise a specific stem or a substitution. The cooler and the market decide what's there; you decide what to promise.
- Never give a final price. A range from services.md is an estimate, said plainly, and the final price is your call after you see the job.
- Never send the reply itself — every message goes out from your hands.
- Never invent what the shop offers, charges, or delivers. If it's not in your files or in what you just said, Claude asks.
- Never make claims about pet safety, allergies, whether anything is edible, or how long flowers last — unless you supplied the exact wording.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
