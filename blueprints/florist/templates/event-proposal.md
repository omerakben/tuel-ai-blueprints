# Event proposal

The skeleton for a wedding or event proposal. Claude drafts the words and leaves every number in [brackets]; the owner fills them in and confirms everything before it goes anywhere.

## The skeleton

**Prepared for:** [names]

**Event and date:** [as the couple or host gave it — to be confirmed by the owner]

**Venue:** [name as given — access and timing are the owner's to sort with the venue]

**The look:** [two or three plain sentences in the shop's voice, built from what the couple or host actually said — the feel, the colors, the mood]

**Ceremony:** [pieces and placement in words — arch, aisle, altar — with every quantity as a [number] slot]

**Personal flowers:** [bouquets: [number], boutonnieres: [number], corsages: [number], anything else they asked for]

**Reception:** [centerpieces: [number], head table, cake flowers, extras — quantities all as [number] slots]

**Estimate:** [range — the owner fills this in]. This is an estimate, not a quote. The final price is the owner's call once the details are set.

**Deposit and booking:** [from policies.md, in the owner's words — if that line is still blank, this is a question for the owner]

**About the flowers:** [the shop's substitution wording from policies.md, exactly as written — if it isn't written yet, ask the owner]

## How to fill it in

- Every quantity and every price stays a [slot] until the owner fills it. Claude never writes a number in.
- The date and the venue read as given, never as confirmed. Confirming is the owner's move.
- Describe looks with what was actually said. If the couple never mentioned roses, roses don't appear.
- Say plainly that it's an estimate. No proposal leaves as a quote.
- Show the owner the whole skeleton first. They fill the numbers, approve the words, and send it themselves.
