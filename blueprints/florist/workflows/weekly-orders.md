# Weekly orders hour

One hour a week to plan the stem buy — Claude lines up the week's orders against the cooler and drafts the order sheets, and you place every order.

## When

Once a week, before your buying day — many owners use the evening before the market run, when the week's orders are mostly in. Say "let's plan the week's orders" and Claude starts.

## Steps

1. Look back. Claude reads last week's orders plan in reports/ and the week's daily closes, and notes what sold out and what sat. If there is no record yet, Claude says so and starts fresh.
2. Gather the week. Claude collects what the week needs: the orders you paste or read out, standing orders from business/clients.md, and any booked event from reports/ whose date falls in the window. Anything it can't see becomes a question, never a guess.
3. Count the cooler. You say what's on hand, in your own words and your own counts. Claude writes it down as you said it and reads it back.
4. Find the gap. Claude lines up what the week needs against what's on hand and lists what's missing, stem by stem. Every line traces to an order or to your count.
5. Sort by supplier. Claude splits the list using business/suppliers.md — market stems, plants, hard goods — and puts anything with no supplier on the question list.
6. Review the sheets. Claude shows you one order sheet per supplier. You cross off, add, or change lines, and Claude updates as you go. Nothing is final until you say it is.
7. Save the plan. With your yes, Claude saves the sheets and the week-at-a-glance as reports/weekly-orders-[date].md. You place the orders yourself — at the market, on the phone, however you always buy.

## What you get

- One order sheet per supplier, ready to take along or read out.
- The week at a glance: which orders and events drove which lines.
- The question list: missing counts, missing suppliers, anything that needs your call.
- All of it saved as reports/weekly-orders-[date].md.

## Never

- Claude never places an order, pays a supplier, or promises a supplier anything. The sheets wait for you.
- Claude never guesses a count — not the cooler's, not an order's. A missing number is a question.
- Claude never treats the market as certain. Availability and prices on the day are whatever you find; the sheet is a plan, not a promise.
- A yes covers this week's sheets, this week. Last week's yes approves nothing today.
- Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
