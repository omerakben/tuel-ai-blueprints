# What to collect, in order

Claude works through this over time — never as a form, always as conversation. Tier 1 is enough for the first win. Everything else can wait.

## Tier 1 — needed for the first win

- Shop name
- What the shop sells most (bouquets, arrangements, plants, weddings and events) and the feel of the place in three words
- Days and opening hours
- Just the owner, or who helps out and on which days
- Go-to offerings with usual price ranges
- How orders reach the shop (walk-ins, calls, messages, a website)
- Whether the shop delivers, roughly how far, and on which days

## Tier 2 — makes it good

- The full price list (photo, file, or pasted — any shape is fine)
- House policies: lead time for custom work, how substitutions are handled, delivery rules, deposits for events, refunds and redos
- The dates that matter: holidays and local events the shop gears up for, so preorder pushes can start two weeks ahead
- The regulars: names the owner wants remembered, their usual order, the dates they order for every year
- Suppliers: the wholesaler or market, order days and cutoffs, who supplies hard goods like vases and ribbon
- Logo and a few photos the owner likes, dropped into `assets/`
- The shop's look and voice: three words, colors, phrases the owner loves, phrases to avoid, and the tags they usually put on posts
- Where reviews land (Google, Yelp) and whether the owner wants help replying

## Tier 3 — nice to have

- What the cooler usually holds, season by season, in the owner's words
- Best sellers and what tends to sell out first
- Busiest and slowest days, as the owner sees them
- The shop's story — how it started, what it's known for
- Wedding and event minimums, only if the owner wants them written down
- The exact wording the owner uses for care notes, pet questions, and how long flowers last — Claude never writes its own
- Anything with a renewal date the owner wants nudges about (licenses, insurance, the van)
