# Plan the week

This playbook turns your bookings and your goals into a simple plan for the week ahead.

## When to run this

At the start of your week, or any time the days ahead feel foggy. Sunday evening
works well, and so does the morning of your first open day. It takes a few
minutes, and it works even if the business/ files are still half full.

## What Claude reads first

- business/profile.md — your hours and the basics
- business/services.md — what you offer and what you charge
- business/staff.md — who is in on which days
- business/policies.md — your deposit and cancellation rules, plus hard limits

And from you: this week's bookings. Paste them from your booking tool, or just
tell Claude how the week looks. A photo of the appointment book works too.

## Steps

1. Claude reads the files above, then asks for this week's bookings if you have
   not shared them yet. Anything unclear, Claude asks instead of guessing.
2. Claude maps the week: the busy stretches, the quiet gaps, and who is in when.
3. Claude flags each quiet spot and suggests one way to fill it — a small offer,
   or a friendly nudge to regulars who have not been in for a while. These are
   ideas on paper, nothing more.
4. If an idea involves messaging anyone or posting anywhere, Claude writes the
   draft, shows it to you, and waits for your go-ahead. You send it yourself,
   from your own accounts, if and when you want to.
5. Claude picks the three things most worth your attention this week and says
   why each one made the list.
6. Claude shows you the whole plan as a draft and asks what to change. Nothing
   is saved until you say it looks right.
7. Once you approve, Claude saves the plan to reports/.

## What you get

A one-page weekly plan saved as reports/week-plan-[date].md, with:

- the shape of your week at a glance
- each quiet spot and one idea for filling it
- who is in when, and any staffing gap to sort out
- your top three for the week

## Never

- Never contact anyone about the plan. Drafts only, and you do the sending.
- Never invent bookings or fill unknowns with guesses. A gap in the information
  means a question for you.
- Never quote a price that is not in business/services.md.
- Never change business/ files while planning. Anything new Claude learned gets
  proposed to you separately, and you decide.

Action level: B — Claude drafts; sending, posting, or paying is always the owner's call.
