# Vision

**Make AI operable by everyone — especially people who have never written a prompt.**

Millions of small-business owners do their marketing, bookkeeping, reminders, and admin at night, after the real work. The tools that could help assume they'll learn prompting, integrations, and dashboards. They won't, and they shouldn't have to.

TUEL AI ships blueprints: downloadable business folders that turn Claude Desktop into a working operator for one specific kind of business, out of the box. The owner downloads their business, opens it, answers a few plain questions, and from that day Claude handles the busywork — always drafting first, always leaving the decisions with the owner.

## The north star

Maria owns a three-chair salon. She downloads the `salon` folder, opens it, and says hello. Twenty minutes later her profile is saved and this week's promo is ready to post. She turns on the morning brief and the end-of-day close. From then on Claude drafts her posts, answers booking questions, reminds clients to rebook, tallies the till and tips, and flags low stock — showing her everything before it goes out. Every blueprint is built to this bar: if it doesn't deliver Maria's first twenty minutes, it doesn't ship.

## Why folders

- **Nothing to install.** A folder is the one software format every owner already understands.
- **The owner keeps the data.** Business memory is plain files on their computer. No account, no upload, no lock-in.
- **Trust is structural.** Draft-first isn't a setting — it is written into every playbook, schedule, and instruction the folder contains.

## How it scales

The `salon` blueprint proves the format. The standard (`standard/SPEC.md`), the blank scaffold (`templates/_blank/`), and the linter make each new vertical a copy-fill-check loop: café, food truck, boutique, gym, cleaning crew, plumber, photographer. Fifty verticals is a library problem, not a research problem.

Blueprints stay ordinary working folders. Packaged Skills or plugins may come later as separate products with their own review — a folder never pretends to be either.

## What success looks like

- Time from download to first accepted deliverable: under thirty minutes.
- The first win requires zero connected tools.
- Owners accept most drafts with light edits — and every edit teaches the folder something they approve.
- The morning brief stays turned on, because it keeps earning its two minutes.
