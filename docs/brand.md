# TUEL AI brand voice

TUEL AI writes for people who run real businesses with their hands and their weekends. The voice is plain, warm, and specific. It sounds like a sharp friend who used to manage a shop, not like software.

## The rules

1. **Write to one owner.** "You", present tense, short sentences. Never "users", "customers", or "businesses" when you mean the person reading.
2. **No tech words in owner-facing files.** These never appear where an owner reads: connector, MCP, schema, API, endpoint, integration, workflow engine, LLM. Say "connect your Gmail", "your booking tool", "Claude".
3. **Say true, concrete things.** "The first session aims to end with a draft you can review" beats any adjective. If a sentence has no fact in it, cut it.
4. **The promise travels with the product.** Wherever we ask for trust, the draft-first promise appears in plain words: you see everything before it goes anywhere.
5. **Calm about safety.** No scary compliance talk and no hand-waving. State "Claude never sends anything without your okay" once, then keep the promise.
6. **Headings in sentence case.** Minimal bold. Do not use em dashes.
7. **Banned:** hype adjectives (seamless, powerful, effortless, game-changing), "not just X, but Y", three-item rhythm lists, and any sentence that would make Maria, a three-chair salon owner, squint. One locked exception: the product tagline "Download your business. Open it. Let Claude run it." stays exactly as it is.

## Vocabulary

| We say (internal) | The owner reads |
|---|---|
| blueprint | your business folder |
| business memory | your business files |
| onboarding agent | the first chat |
| operating agent | your assistant |
| skill | playbook |
| schedule | the morning brief, the end-of-day close |
| connector recommendation | tools you can connect (all optional) |

## The test

Read it aloud as if to a salon owner at 7pm on a Tuesday who has been on her feet since eight. If she'd say "okay, got it", ship it. If she'd say "what does that mean?", rewrite it.
